\begin{abstract}
The Stringer bound is widely used to evaluate monetary-unit samples in
auditing, but its finite-sample coverage has not been established in
general. We prove that, for sample size $n=2$, the Stringer bound with
binomial factors is conservative for every distribution on $[0,1]$ and
every nominal confidence level. The result is sharp: the supremum of the
noncoverage probability is $\alpha$, although no distribution attains
it. We also give explicit finite-sample counterexamples at larger sample
sizes and low nominal confidence levels. At nominal confidence levels
from $30\%$ to $37\%$ and sample sizes from $50$ to $400$, exact rational
certificates establish coverage below nominal. In searches at $50\%$,
$90\%$, and $95\%$, we found no violations; the smallest coverage found
agreed with nominal coverage to within $10^{-10}$. Finally, we revisit a
previous finite-sample certification and identify an index shift in its
band-containment argument. The corrected containment event has
probability at most $1-\alpha$, so that argument cannot establish the
desired coverage guarantee. These results establish distribution-free
finite-sample conservatism at $n=2$, provide explicit certified failures
at larger $n$, and sharpen the remaining conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} monetary-unit sampling; Stringer bound;\\
finite-sample coverage; bounded means; Clopper--Pearson limits.

\section{Introduction}

Monetary-unit sampling (MUS) draws $n$ dollar units at random from an
audited population. The \emph{taint} of a drawn unit is $T\in[0,1]$,
the overstated fraction of the account item associated with that dollar.
With taints $t_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge t_{(n)}$ arranged in nonincreasing
order, the Stringer bound \cite{stringer1963} at nominal confidence
$1-\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sb}
\SB \;=\; p_n(0) \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(p_n(j)-p_n(j-1)\bigr)\,t_{(j)},
\end{equation}
where $p_n(j)$ is the upper Clopper--Pearson limit for $j$ successes in
$n$ trials, i.e.\ the root of $\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{n}{i}p^i(1-p)^{n-i}
=\alpha$, with $p_n(n)=1$. Audit guidance also tabulates Poisson-based
confidence factors for MUS evaluation \cite[Appendix~C]{aicpa2012}; the
corresponding limits dominate the binomial ones. The method's justification has
always been the belief that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
\pr\bigl(\SB \ge \mu(F)\bigr) \;\ge\; 1-\alpha
\qquad\text{for all } n \text{ and all } F \text{ on } [0,1],
\end{equation}
where $T_1,\dots,T_n$ are drawn independently from the common
distribution $F$, with mean $\mu(F)$. This is the standard i.i.d.\ model
used in published analyses of the bound. A National Research Council
panel noted that the formulation of the Stringer bound had not been
satisfactorily explained \cite{panel1989}. The principal prior results
are as follows. Bickel \cite{bickel1992} proved
\eqref{eq:conj} for $n=1$, gave the lower bound $(1-\alpha)^{n+1}$ for
$n\ge2$ under conditions on $F$, and derived the expansion
$\SB = \bar T + c(F)z_{1-\alpha}n^{-1/2}+o_p(n^{-1/2})$ with
$c^2(F)\ge\operatorname{Var}(T)$; Pap and van Zuijlen
\cite{papvz1996} made the expansion almost sure and concluded that the
bound is asymptotically conservative for $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12]$ and
asymptotically \emph{anti}-conservative for $\alpha\in(\tfrac12,1)$, and
in \cite{papvz1995} exhibited non-conservatism for uniform-type taints at
sub-$50\%$ confidence via exact recursions. De Jager, Pap and van
Zuijlen \cite{djpvz1997} proved that on $\{0,1\}$-supported taints the
bound reduces to Clopper--Pearson (where \eqref{eq:conj} holds exactly)
and that Stringer's coefficients are minimal with that property. A 2021
survey of confidence bounds for bounded means categorized the Stringer
bound among methods conjectured to have guaranteed coverage and
described its coverage for $\alpha<0.5$ as unknown
\cite[p.~8569]{phan2021}.

This paper makes three contributions.

\emph{(1) A theorem at $n=2$} (Section~\ref{sec:thm}). The conjecture
\eqref{eq:conj} holds at $n=2$ for every $F$ and every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, including $\alpha>1/2$. Thus the known asymptotic
failures do not occur at $n=2$, and the result is sharp. To our
knowledge, this is the first distribution-free finite-sample proof for
$n=2$.

\emph{(2) Exact counterexamples at low confidence}
(Section~\ref{sec:counter}). Thirty-three certified three-point
distributions, each with two nonzero taint values and an atom at zero,
give counterexamples at confidence levels $30\%$--$37\%$ and sample
sizes $n=50$--$400$. Their coverage is evaluated in exact rational
arithmetic with margin certificates. In searches at $50\%$, $90\%$,
and $95\%$, no violation was found. At every sample size searched, the
smallest coverage found agreed with the nominal level to within
$10^{-10}$.

\emph{(3) Reassessment of a previous certification}
(Section~\ref{sec:bimpeh}). A proposed finite-sample certification
\cite{bimpeh2008} contains an index shift in its band-containment
argument. We give hand-checkable examples for which the stated
containment inequality fails and examine the corrected event.

\section{Finite-sample conservatism at \texorpdfstring{$n=2$}{n=2}}\label{sec:thm}

For $n=2$ the factors are explicit: $p_2(0)=1-\sqrt{\alpha}$,
$p_2(1)=\sqrt{1-\alpha}$, $p_2(2)=1$. Write
\[
\beta=\sqrt{\alpha},\quad \gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\quad(\beta^2+\gamma^2=1),
\qquad A = \beta+\gamma-1,\quad B=1-\gamma,
\]
so $A,B>0$ and $A+B=\beta$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n2}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and let $T_1,T_2$ be i.i.d.\ with common
distribution $F$ on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. Then
$\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge 1-\alpha$. Moreover
$\sup_F \pr(\SB<\mu)=\alpha$, and the supremum is not attained.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Reduction}

Substituting $U_i=1-T_i$, $w=1-\mu=\E[U]$, $m=\min(U_1,U_2)$,
$M=\max(U_1,U_2)$ into \eqref{eq:sb} and telescoping gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:red}
\SB \;=\; 1-\bigl(A\,m+B\,M\bigr),
\end{equation}
so $\SB\ge\mu\iff Am+BM\le w$. The theorem is implied by the
\emph{wedge inequality}: for $U_1,U_2$ i.i.d.\ on $[0,1]$ with mean
$w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge}
\pr\bigl(Am+BM< w\bigr)\;\ge\;\gamma^2.
\end{equation}
(If $w=0$ then $U\equiv0$ a.s.\ and $\SB=1\ge\mu$ almost surely. The strict
form \eqref{eq:wedge} is in fact equivalent to the theorem, by applying
the weak form to $(1-\varepsilon)U$ and letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$; only the stated implication is used.)

If $w>\beta$ the event $\{Am+BM\ge w\}$ is empty, since the left side
is at most $A+B=\beta$; if $w=\beta$ it is exactly $\{U_1=U_2=1\}$
(as $A,B>0$), of probability $\pr(U=1)^2\le w^2=\alpha$ by Markov's
inequality.

Henceforth, assume $0<w<\beta$. The proof has three steps. First, the
rectangle lemma converts a hypothetical failure of the wedge inequality
into pointwise lower bounds on the survival function $G$. Second,
boundary rectangles relate values of $G$ on opposite sides of $u^*$.
Third, the potential inequality combines those bounds and forces the
integral of $G$ to exceed its prescribed value $w$, producing a
contradiction.

\subsection{Rectangle bounds}

Let $F(x)=\pr(U\le x)$, $G=1-F$, and recall $w=\int_0^1G$. Define
\[
u^*=\frac{w}{\beta}\in(0,1),\qquad
a_0=\max\Bigl(0,\frac{w-B}{A}\Bigr),\qquad
T^\dagger=\min\Bigl(\frac{w}{B},1\Bigr),
\]
and note $a_0<u^*<T^\dagger$.

\begin{lemma}[Rectangle lemma]\label{lem:rect}
If $0\le a\le b\le 1$ and $Aa+Bb<w$, then
$\pr(Am+BM<w)\ge 2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $\{U_1\le a,U_2\le b\}\cup\{U_1\le b,U_2\le a\}$ we have $m\le a$,
$M\le b$, hence $Am+BM\le Aa+Bb<w$ (using $A,B\ge0$). By
inclusion--exclusion (the intersection is $\{U_1\le a, U_2\le a\}$
since $a\le b$) the union has probability $2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{proof}

Assume for contradiction that \eqref{eq:wedge} fails. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
\tag{H}
2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2<\gamma^2
\quad\text{whenever } 0\le a\le b\le 1,\; Aa+Bb<w.
\end{equation}
Three families of admissible rectangles give pointwise bounds on $G$:

\emph{(i) Squares.} For $t<u^*$, $(a,b)=(t,t)$ gives $F(t)^2<\gamma^2$,
i.e.\ $G(t)>1-\gamma=B$ for all $t\in[0,u^*)$.

\emph{(ii) Full-height rectangles} (nonvacuous if and only if $w>B$).
For $a<a_0$,
$(a,1)$ gives $1-G(a)^2<\gamma^2$, i.e.\ $G(a)>\beta$ on $[0,a_0)$.

\emph{(iii) Boundary pairs.} Let $h(g)=\dfrac{\beta^2-g^2}{2(1-g)}$ on
$[0,1)$. For $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ set $\bar a(b)=(w-Bb)/A\in
(a_0,u^*)$. Every $a'<\bar a(b)$ is admissible with $b$; letting
$a'\uparrow\bar a(b)$ and writing $g=G(\bar a(b)^-)<1$,
inequality \eqref{eq:H} rearranges to
\[
G(b)\;\ge\;h\bigl(G(\bar a(b)^-)\bigr)
\qquad\text{for all } b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)
\]
because $2(1-g)-\gamma^2-(1-g)^2=\beta^2-g^2$. If
$g=1$ the bound $G(b)\ge0$ is used instead, consistent with the
convention $h_+(1)=0$ below.

\subsection{Potential function and budget}

The function $\tilde k$ below is chosen to combine the contribution of
$G(a)$ with the corresponding lower bound for $G(b(a))$ after the
change of variables used below. Let $h_+=\max(h,0)$, extended
continuously by $h_+(1)=0$, and define
\[
\tilde k(g)\;=\;g+\frac{A}{B}\,h_+(g),\qquad g\in(B,1].
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pot}
$\tilde k(g)\ge\beta$ on $(B,1]$, with equality only at $g=\beta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\beta^2-B^2=\alpha-(1-\gamma)^2=2\gamma(1-\gamma)$, we get
$h(B)=B$, hence $\tilde k(B^+)\to B+\frac{A}{B}B=A+B=\beta$; also
$h(\beta)=0$ so $\tilde k(\beta)=\beta$. Differentiating,
$h'(g)=\frac{(1-g)^2-\gamma^2}{2(1-g)^2}$, so $h$ has its unique
critical point at $g=B$ (where $(1-g)^2=\gamma^2$) and decreases on
$(B,1)$; hence $h\ge0$ on $[B,\beta]$ (it decreases from $B>0$ to $0$)
and $h<0$ on $(\beta,1)$. Further
$h''(g)=-\gamma^2/(1-g)^3<0$: $h$, and therefore
$\tilde k=g+\frac{A}{B}h$, is strictly concave on $[B,\beta]$. A
strictly concave function with equal endpoint values $\beta$ satisfies
$\tilde k\ge\beta$ on $[B,\beta]$, strictly on the interior. On
$(\beta,1]$, $h_+=0$ and $\tilde k(g)=g>\beta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:n2}]
Assume \eqref{eq:H}. The substitution $t=b(a)=(w-Aa)/B$ is a
decreasing bijection from $[a_0,u^*]$ onto $[u^*,T^\dagger]$, with
$|dt|=(A/B)\,da$ (endpoints: $b(u^*)=u^*$; $b(a_0)=1=T^\dagger$ if
$w\ge B$, $b(0)=w/B=T^\dagger$ if $w\le B$). Using
$w=\int_0^1G\ge\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G+\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G$,
bound the tail by (iii) (the left-limit $G(a^-)$ agrees with $G(a)$
for all but countably many $a$, so the integrals coincide):
\[
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G
=\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G(b(a))\,da
\;\ge\;\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}h_+\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da.
\]
By (i), $G>B$ pointwise on $[a_0,u^*)$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:pot} applies
under the integral:
\[
w\;\ge\;\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}\tilde k\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da
\;\ge\;\beta a_0+\beta(u^*-a_0)\;=\;\beta u^*\;=\;w,
\]
where $\int_0^{a_0}G\ge\beta a_0$ by (ii). Equality is forced
throughout; we exhibit a strict inequality in every case:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $a_0>0$: (ii) is pointwise strict on $[0,a_0)$, so
$\int_0^{a_0}G>\beta a_0$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G\ne\beta$ on a positive-measure subset of
$(0,u^*)$: there $\tilde k(G)>\beta$ strictly
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}), so $\int_0^{u^*}\tilde k(G)>\beta u^*$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G=\beta$ a.e.\ on $(0,u^*)$: for each
$b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ choose $a'<\bar a(b)$ with $G(a')=\beta$
(possible, as such $a'$ have full measure in a nonempty interval);
the \emph{strict} inequality \eqref{eq:H} at the admissible corner
$(a',b)$ reads $(1-\beta)(1+\beta-2G(b))<\gamma^2=(1-\beta)(1+\beta)$,
forcing $G(b)>0$ for every $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$; hence
$\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>0$ and $w\ge\int_0^{u^*}G+
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>\beta u^*=w$.
\end{itemize}
Each case yields $w>w$, a contradiction. Thus \eqref{eq:H} is
impossible, proving
\eqref{eq:wedge} and the coverage statement.

\emph{Sharpness.} Distributions supported on $\{0,u\}$, with
$F=(1-q)\delta_0+q\delta_u$, give failure probability $q^2$ when
$q<\beta$ (the pair $(u,u)$, since
$\beta u> qu=w$) and additionally the mixed pairs when $q<B$
(since $Bu>qu$), totalling $1-(1-q)^2$; letting $q\uparrow\beta$ and
$q\uparrow B$ respectively yields failure probabilities approaching
$\beta^2=\alpha$ and $1-\gamma^2=\alpha$. Hence $\sup_F\pr(\SB<\mu)
=\alpha$.

\emph{Non-attainment.} If some $F$ attained it, the weak form of
\eqref{eq:H} would hold, and repeating the integral argument with weak
inequalities forces $G=\beta$ a.e.\ on $(0,u^*)$ (by strictness of
$\tilde k$ off $\beta$) and $G=0$ a.e.\ beyond $u^*$; right-continuous
monotonicity then forces $F=(1-\beta)\delta_0+\beta\delta_{u^*}$, whose
largest value of $Am+BM$ is $\beta u^*=w$ exactly, so
$\pr(Am+BM>w)=0\ne\alpha$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to known inequalities]\label{rem:known}
The equal-weights case of \eqref{eq:wedge}, which bounds
$\pr(X_1+X_2\ge t)$ for nonnegative i.i.d.\ variables with a given
mean, is the classical sharp inequality of Hoeffding and Shrikhande
\cite{hs1955} (extended to general $k$ in \cite{lms2016}); the wedge
inequality is its order-statistic-weighted analogue, with genuinely
unequal weights $A\ne B$ for all $\alpha\in(0,1)$. The sharp-bound
literature for order statistics and L-statistics under moment
conditions (see \cite{balrych2024}) concerns expectations, or
distribution functions of single order statistics, not
mean-constrained tail probabilities of weighted combinations; we are
not aware of a prior result implying \eqref{eq:wedge}. Other
distribution-free upper bounds for bounded means include those of
Anderson \cite{anderson1969} and Gaffke \cite{gaffke2005}; the latter's
finite-sample validity for independent variables was proved recently
\cite{vlassis2026}. Those results are not used here, and the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} is self-contained.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}
For $n=2$ the Stringer bound with Poisson (AICPA) factors is
conservative for every $F$ and every $\alpha$ for which the Poisson
factors dominate the binomial ones; the domination is verified with
rigorous enclosures for standard levels in the repository.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[One nonzero taint value]\label{prop:twopoint}
If $F$ is supported on $\{0,v\}$ with $v\in(0,1]$, then, for every
$n$ and $\alpha$, $\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $K$ the number of nonzero taints, $K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)$,
$\SB=(1-v)p_n(0)+v\,p_n(K)\ge v\,p_n(K)$ and $\mu=qv$; on the
Clopper--Pearson coverage event $\{p_n(K)\ge q\}$, of probability at
least $1-\alpha$, we get $\SB\ge vq=\mu$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact counterexamples at low confidence}\label{sec:counter}

Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} covers every confidence level, so the asymptotic
anti-conservatism at $\alpha>1/2$ \cite{papvz1996} must manifest only at
larger $n$. We give explicit finite-sample counterexamples. Each
counterexample below is certified: its distributional parameters are
exact dyadic rationals, its coverage is computed as an exact rational by
full multinomial enumeration, and a margin certificate verifies that no
comparison $\SB\ge\mu$ was decided within the numerical error of the
confidence factors, which are enclosed in bisection brackets of width
$10^{-40}$.

A representative certificate has nominal confidence $30\%$
($\alpha=0.7$) and $n=50$. Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=1,
&q_1&=\frac{8984394653678481}{2^{55}},\\[5pt]
v_2&=\frac{8384891858230937}{2^{54}},
&q_2&=\frac{5642023711000999}{2^{53}},\\[5pt]
&&q_0&=\frac{4476307521281491}{2^{55}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The complete distribution is
$\pr(T=v_1)=q_1$, $\pr(T=v_2)=q_2$, and $\pr(T=0)=q_0$.
Thus the two nonzero taint values are $1$ and approximately $0.465455$,
and the corresponding probabilities are approximately $0.249367$ and
$0.626390$. The exact rational coverage, printed in full in the third
record of the
\href{https://github.com/RichieSater/stringer-bound/blob/v1.0.0/supporting-materials/computations/certificates/certified-alpha0.7-n50.json}{archived $n=50$, $\alpha=0.7$ certificate}
(\path{certified-alpha0.7-n50.json}),
is approximately $0.2988790828<0.30$. The minimum comparison margin is
approximately $2.10\times10^{-14}$, against a factor-error bound of
approximately $4.68\times10^{-39}$. Pap and van Zuijlen
\cite{papvz1995} established non-conservatism for uniform-type taints
through exact recursions; the finite-support examples here are readily
reproducible and illustrate the onset of failure in the examined
parameter ranges.

By contrast, searches at nominal confidence levels $50\%$, $90\%$, and
$95\%$ found no violation. Grid-plus-optimizer searches covered
distributions with two nonzero taint values ($n\le100$ at $95\%$ and
$90\%$; $n\le300$ at $50\%$) and distributions with three nonzero taint
values ($n\le25$ at $95\%$), in each case allowing mass at zero. At every
sample size searched, the smallest coverage found agreed with the
nominal level to within $10^{-10}$. Near-minimizers occurred as the
largest taint approached $1$, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:twopoint}, the $\{0,1\}$-support minimality result
of \cite{djpvz1997}, and a conjecture that is exactly tight. For the
Poisson factors, the smallest coverage found in the corresponding
searches remained above nominal. At $95\%$, for example, the values were
approximately $0.9716$ ($n=10$), $0.9573$ ($n=50$), and $0.9549$
($n=100$), a positive numerical cushion that decreases over the listed
sample sizes.

\section{Reassessment of a previous finite-sample certification}\label{sec:bimpeh}

Let
\[
C_n(F)=\pr_F\bigl(\SB\ge\mu(F)\bigr)
\]
denote the coverage probability. In this section,
$t_{1:n}\le\cdots\le t_{n:n}$ are the sample taints arranged in
nondecreasing order, with $t_{0:n}=0$ and $t_{n+1:n}=1$. Let $q_n(i)$
be the lower one-sided Clopper--Pearson limit for $i$ successes, with
$q_n(0)=0$. The lower and upper limits used here satisfy
$q_n(i)=1-p_n(n-i)$.

Bimpeh \cite[pp.~76--79]{bimpeh2008} writes the Stringer bound as
$\SB=\int_0^1[1-\widehat F_{n,L}(t)]\,dt$, where the stepwise band is
defined by
\[
\widehat F_{n,L}(t)=q_n(i-1)
\quad\text{for }t_{i-1:n}\le t<t_{i:n},\qquad 1\le i\le n+1.
\]
Let $V_{1:n}\le\cdots\le V_{n:n}$ denote the order statistics of $n$
independent $\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$ random variables. Using the
probability integral transform and the reversal symmetry of uniform
order statistics, Bimpeh proposes the lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bimpeh}
C_n(F)\;\ge\;\bar P_n
\;:=\;\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\le p_n(i),\ 1\le i\le n\bigr).
\end{equation}
The value $\bar P_n$ is evaluated with Bolshev's recursion for uniform
order-statistic boundary-crossing probabilities
\cite[pp.~366--367]{shorackwellner1986}. Because the computed values
satisfy $\bar P_n\ge1-\alpha$ for $n\le11$ when $\alpha=0.05$, the
thesis concludes that \eqref{eq:conj} holds in that range. It also notes
that
\[
\bar P_2=2\sqrt{1-\alpha}-(1-\alpha)\ge1-\alpha
\]
for every $\alpha$ and claims a proof for $n\le2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bimpehwrong}
Inequality \eqref{eq:bimpeh} fails for both continuous and atomic
distributions $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha=0.05$ and let $F$ be the mixture assigning probability
$0.99$ to a uniform distribution on $(0.99,1)$ and probability $0.01$
to a uniform distribution on $(0,0.01)$, so $\mu=0.9851$. For $n=1$,
$\SB=0.95+0.05\,T_1$ and $\bar P_1=1$. Moreover,
$\SB<\mu$ if and only if $T_1<0.702$, an event of probability $0.01$.
Thus $C_1(F)=0.99<1=\bar P_1$. For $n=2$,
$\bar P_2=2\sqrt{0.95}-0.95\approx0.99936$. A sample containing one
taint from each mixture component fails, as does a sample containing
two taints from the lower component. Therefore
$C_2(F)=1-2(0.99)(0.01)-0.01^2=0.9801<\bar P_2$.

For an atomic example at $n=5$, let
$\pr(T=1)=\tfrac12$ and
$\pr(T=\tfrac1{10})=\pr(T=0)=\tfrac14$. Its exact coverage is
$C_5(F)=\tfrac{31}{32}=0.96875<\bar P_5\approx0.98746$. The failure
event is precisely the event of drawing no taint-$1$ unit, which has
probability $2^{-5}$.
\end{proof}

The issue is an index shift. Pointwise domination
$F\ge\widehat F_{n,L}$ on $[0,1]$ requires
$F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)$ for all $i$, because $q_n(i)$ is the band's
value at the jump point $t_{i:n}$. The cited argument instead uses the
left limit $q_n(i-1)$. In particular, it omits the constraint
$F(t_{n:n})\ge q_n(n)=\alpha^{1/n}$. Correcting the index does not
recover the claimed coverage guarantee.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cannot}
For every continuous $F$ and every $n$, the corrected containment
probability satisfies
$\pr\bigl(F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)
=\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)\le1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For continuous $F$, the probability integral transform gives
$F(t_{i:n})=V_{i:n}$ in distribution. The corrected event includes its
$i=n$ constraint, and $q_n(n)=\alpha^{1/n}$. Hence
\[
\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)
\le \pr\bigl(V_{n:n}\ge\alpha^{1/n}\bigr)=1-\alpha,
\]
because $\pr(V_{n:n}<x)=x^n$.
\end{proof}

Thus the corrected pointwise-containment event has probability no
greater than nominal coverage. The integral inequality
$\int\widehat F_{n,L}\le\int F$ may hold on a larger event than
pointwise domination, but the cited containment argument does not
establish that fact. We reproduce the values of $\bar P_n$ and
Bimpeh's Table~5.1 exactly; those values are boundary-crossing
probabilities, not established coverage bounds. Consequently, the cited
argument does not establish finite-sample conservatism at $n=2$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} supplies that result directly.

\section{Discussion}

Three observations suggest directions for future work. First, the reformulation behind
\eqref{eq:red} generalizes: with $u_{(1)}\le\cdots\le u_{(n)}$ the
increasing order statistics of $U_i=1-T_i$,
\[
\SB\;=\;1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_j\,u_{(j)},\qquad
e_j=p_n(j)-p_n(j-1),
\]
so \eqref{eq:conj} is a family of weighted order-statistic exceedance
inequalities, with all audit content absorbed into the decreasing
weights $e_j$. Second, the $n=2$ proof combines rectangle lower bounds
on the covering event, a survival-integral budget, and a concave
potential with equal endpoint values. This architecture does not
obviously depend on $n=2$ except through the explicit factor algebra; the
$n\ge3$ factors lack closed forms, so the endpoint identities
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}) would need structural rather than algebraic
proofs. Third, the numerical sharpness observed at every searched $n$
and the certified failures at several confidence levels below $50\%$
suggest that \eqref{eq:conj} may hold for $\alpha\le1/2$, with coverage
infimum $1-\alpha$ at every $n$. This is a concrete conjecture for
future work.

From an auditing perspective, the binomial Stringer bound has no uniform
safety margin above nominal coverage: at $n=2$, its coverage infimum is
$1-\alpha$. The Poisson factors examined here retain a positive but
decreasing numerical cushion. The certified examples also show that
anti-conservatism can occur at several nominal confidence levels below
$50\%$ and at sample sizes between $50$ and $400$.
