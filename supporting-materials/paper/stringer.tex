\begin{abstract}
The Stringer bound (Stringer, 1963) is the standard evaluation of
monetary-unit samples in auditing. Its defining property --- that its
finite-sample coverage is at least the nominal confidence level --- has
remained unproven for six decades: Bickel (1992) and Pap and van Zuijlen
(1996) established asymptotic conservatism for $\alpha\le 1/2$ and
asymptotic anti-conservatism for $\alpha>1/2$, but no finite-sample proof
beyond $n=1$ exists. We contribute three results. First, we prove the
conjecture at $n=2$: for every taint distribution on $[0,1]$ and
\emph{every} confidence level, the Stringer bound with binomial factors is
conservative, and the bound is sharp (the supremum of the failure
probability equals $\alpha$, approached but not attained). To our
knowledge this is the first finite-sample proof at any $n\ge 2$ free of
hypotheses on the distribution; a second, structurally different proof of
the key inequality is sketched. Second, we exhibit the first exact
(non-simulation) finite-sample counterexamples at explicit parameters:
certified in rational arithmetic, coverage falls below the nominal level
at confidence levels $30\%$--$37\%$ for $n$ between $50$ and $400$, with
the shortfall growing in $n$ --- while at $50\%$ and above no violation is
found, with the searched infimum equal to the nominal level exactly.
Third, we refute the only claimed finite-sample certification in the
literature (Bimpeh, 2008), which asserted conservatism for all $F$ at
$n\le 11$ for $\alpha=0.05$ and claimed a proof for $n\le2$: its
band-containment inequality fails for continuous and atomic
distributions alike, and the corrected containment probability can
never exceed $1-\alpha$, so the method could not have certified
conservatism at any $n$ --- making the present theorem the first
correct finite-sample proof at $n=2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Monetary-unit sampling (MUS) draws $n$ dollar units at random from an
audited population; the \emph{taint} of a drawn unit is
$T\in[0,1]$, the overstated fraction of the line item owning that
dollar. With taints $t_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge t_{(n)}$ sorted decreasingly,
the Stringer bound at nominal confidence $1-\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sb}
\SB \;=\; p_n(0) \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(p_n(j)-p_n(j-1)\bigr)\,t_{(j)},
\end{equation}
where $p_n(j)$ is the upper Clopper--Pearson limit for $j$ successes in
$n$ trials, i.e.\ the root of $\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{n}{i}p^i(1-p)^{n-i}
=\alpha$, with $p_n(n)=1$; audit practice frequently substitutes Poisson
limits, which dominate the binomial ones. The method's justification has
always been the belief that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
\pr\bigl(\SB \ge \mu(F)\bigr) \;\ge\; 1-\alpha
\qquad\text{for all } n \text{ and all } F \text{ on } [0,1],
\end{equation}
where $T_1,\dots,T_n$ are i.i.d.\ $F$ with mean $\mu(F)$ --- the standard
i.i.d.\ model in which all published analyses of the bound are set. As
the National Research Council's panel observed, ``the formulation of the
Stringer bound has never been satisfactorily explained''
\cite{panel1989}. What is known: Bickel \cite{bickel1992} proved
\eqref{eq:conj} for $n=1$, gave the lower bound $(1-\alpha)^{n+1}$ for
$n\ge2$ under conditions on $F$, and derived the expansion
$\SB = \bar T + c(F)z_{1-\alpha}n^{-1/2}+o_p(n^{-1/2})$ with
$c^2(F)\ge\operatorname{Var}(T)$; Pap and van Zuijlen
\cite{papvz1996} made the expansion almost sure and concluded that the
bound is asymptotically conservative for $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12]$ and
asymptotically \emph{anti}-conservative for $\alpha\in(\tfrac12,1)$, and
in \cite{papvz1995} exhibited non-conservatism for uniform-type taints at
sub-$50\%$ confidence via exact recursions. De Jager, Pap and van
Zuijlen \cite{djpvz1997} proved that on $\{0,1\}$-supported taints the
bound reduces to Clopper--Pearson (where \eqref{eq:conj} holds exactly)
and that Stringer's coefficients are minimal with that property. A 2021
survey of confidence bounds for bounded means lists the Stringer bound
among ``bounds conjectured to have guaranteed coverage,'' its coverage
for $\alpha<0.5$ ``unknown'' \cite{phan2021}.

This paper makes three contributions.

\emph{(1) A theorem at $n=2$} (Section~\ref{sec:thm}): the conjecture
\eqref{eq:conj} holds at $n=2$ for every $F$ and every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$ --- including $\alpha>1/2$, so the known asymptotic
failures are strictly a large-$n$ phenomenon --- and it is sharp.

\emph{(2) Exact counterexamples at low confidence}
(Section~\ref{sec:counter}): thirty-three certified two-atom
counterexamples at confidence levels $30\%$--$37\%$, $n=50$--$400$,
evaluated in exact rational arithmetic with margin certificates; and
exhaustive-search evidence that at $90\%$, $95\%$, and $50\%$ the
infimum over two- and three-atom populations equals the nominal level
exactly at every $n$ searched.

\emph{(3) A correction to the record} (Section~\ref{sec:bimpeh}): the
only claimed finite-sample certification beyond $n\le2$
\cite{bimpeh2008} rests on an index error; we refute its central
inequality by hand-checkable counterexamples and prove that its method,
corrected, cannot certify conservatism at any $n$.

\section{The theorem at $n=2$}\label{sec:thm}

For $n=2$ the factors are explicit: $p_2(0)=1-\sqrt{\alpha}$,
$p_2(1)=\sqrt{1-\alpha}$, $p_2(2)=1$. Write
\[
\beta=\sqrt{\alpha},\quad \gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\quad(\beta^2+\gamma^2=1),
\qquad A = \beta+\gamma-1,\quad B=1-\gamma,
\]
so $A,B>0$ and $A+B=\beta$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n2}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and let $T_1,T_2$ be i.i.d.\ from any distribution
$F$ on $[0,1]$ with mean $\mu$. Then
$\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge 1-\alpha$. Moreover
$\sup_F \pr(\SB<\mu)=\alpha$, and the supremum is not attained.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Reduction}

Substituting $U_i=1-T_i$, $w=1-\mu=\E[U]$, $m=\min(U_1,U_2)$,
$M=\max(U_1,U_2)$ into \eqref{eq:sb} and telescoping gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:red}
\SB \;=\; 1-\bigl(A\,m+B\,M\bigr),
\end{equation}
so $\SB\ge\mu\iff Am+BM\le w$. The theorem is implied by the
\emph{wedge inequality}: for $U_1,U_2$ i.i.d.\ on $[0,1]$ with mean
$w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge}
\pr\bigl(Am+BM< w\bigr)\;\ge\;\gamma^2 .
\end{equation}
(If $w=0$ then $U\equiv0$ a.s.\ and $\SB=1\ge\mu$ surely. The strict
form \eqref{eq:wedge} is in fact equivalent to the theorem, by applying
the weak form to $(1-\varepsilon)U$ and letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$; only the stated implication is used.)

If $w>\beta$ the event $\{Am+BM\ge w\}$ is empty, since the left side
is at most $A+B=\beta$; if $w=\beta$ it is exactly $\{U_1=U_2=1\}$
(as $A,B>0$), of probability $\pr(U=1)^2\le w^2=\alpha$ by Markov's
inequality. \textbf{Assume henceforth $0<w<\beta$.}

\subsection{Rectangle bounds}

Let $F(x)=\pr(U\le x)$, $G=1-F$, and recall $w=\int_0^1G$. Define
\[
u^*=\frac{w}{\beta}\in(0,1),\qquad
a_0=\max\Bigl(0,\frac{w-B}{A}\Bigr),\qquad
T^\dagger=\min\Bigl(\frac{w}{B},1\Bigr),
\]
and note $a_0<u^*<T^\dagger$.

\begin{lemma}[Rectangle lemma]\label{lem:rect}
If $0\le a\le b\le 1$ and $Aa+Bb<w$, then
$\pr(Am+BM<w)\ge 2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $\{U_1\le a,U_2\le b\}\cup\{U_1\le b,U_2\le a\}$ we have $m\le a$,
$M\le b$, hence $Am+BM\le Aa+Bb<w$ (using $A,B\ge0$). By
inclusion--exclusion (the intersection is $\{U_1\le a, U_2\le a\}$
since $a\le b$) the union has probability $2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{proof}

Assume for contradiction that \eqref{eq:wedge} fails. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
\tag{H}
2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2<\gamma^2
\quad\text{whenever } 0\le a\le b\le 1,\; Aa+Bb<w .
\end{equation}
Three families of admissible rectangles give pointwise bounds on $G$:

\emph{(i) Squares.} For $t<u^*$, $(a,b)=(t,t)$ gives $F(t)^2<\gamma^2$,
i.e.\ $G(t)>1-\gamma=B$ for all $t\in[0,u^*)$.

\emph{(ii) Full-height rectangles} (nonvacuous iff $w>B$). For $a<a_0$,
$(a,1)$ gives $1-G(a)^2<\gamma^2$, i.e.\ $G(a)>\beta$ on $[0,a_0)$.

\emph{(iii) Boundary pairs.} Let $h(g)=\dfrac{\beta^2-g^2}{2(1-g)}$ on
$[0,1)$. For $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ set $\bar a(b)=(w-Bb)/A\in
(a_0,u^*)$. Every $a'<\bar a(b)$ is admissible with $b$; letting
$a'\uparrow\bar a(b)$ and writing $g=G(\bar a(b)^-)<1$,
inequality \eqref{eq:H} rearranges to
\[
G(b)\;\ge\;h\bigl(G(\bar a(b)^-)\bigr)
\qquad\text{for all } b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)
\]
(the numerator algebra: $2(1-g)-\gamma^2-(1-g)^2=\beta^2-g^2$). If
$g=1$ the bound $G(b)\ge0$ is used instead, consistent with the
convention $h_+(1)=0$ below.

\subsection{Potential function and budget}

Let $h_+=\max(h,0)$, extended continuously by $h_+(1)=0$, and define
\[
\tilde k(g)\;=\;g+\frac{A}{B}\,h_+(g),\qquad g\in(B,1].
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pot}
$\tilde k(g)\ge\beta$ on $(B,1]$, with equality only at $g=\beta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\beta^2-B^2=\alpha-(1-\gamma)^2=2\gamma(1-\gamma)$, we get
$h(B)=B$, hence $\tilde k(B^+)\to B+\frac{A}{B}B=A+B=\beta$; also
$h(\beta)=0$ so $\tilde k(\beta)=\beta$. Differentiating,
$h'(g)=\frac{(1-g)^2-\gamma^2}{2(1-g)^2}$, so $h$ has its unique
critical point at $g=B$ (where $(1-g)^2=\gamma^2$) and decreases on
$(B,1)$; hence $h\ge0$ on $[B,\beta]$ (it decreases from $B>0$ to $0$)
and $h<0$ on $(\beta,1)$. Further
$h''(g)=-\gamma^2/(1-g)^3<0$: $h$, and therefore
$\tilde k=g+\frac{A}{B}h$, is strictly concave on $[B,\beta]$. A
strictly concave function with equal endpoint values $\beta$ satisfies
$\tilde k\ge\beta$ on $[B,\beta]$, strictly on the interior. On
$(\beta,1]$, $h_+=0$ and $\tilde k(g)=g>\beta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:n2}]
Assume \eqref{eq:H}. The substitution $t=b(a)=(w-Aa)/B$ maps
$[a_0,u^*]$ decreasingly onto $[u^*,T^\dagger]$ with
$|dt|=(A/B)\,da$ (endpoints: $b(u^*)=u^*$; $b(a_0)=1=T^\dagger$ if
$w\ge B$, $b(0)=w/B=T^\dagger$ if $w\le B$). Using
$w=\int_0^1G\ge\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G+\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G$,
bound the tail by (iii) (the left-limit $G(a^-)$ agrees with $G(a)$
for all but countably many $a$, so the integrals coincide):
\[
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G
=\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G(b(a))\,da
\;\ge\;\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}h_+\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da .
\]
By (i), $G>B$ pointwise on $[a_0,u^*)$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:pot} applies
under the integral:
\[
w\;\ge\;\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}\tilde k\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da
\;\ge\;\beta a_0+\beta(u^*-a_0)\;=\;\beta u^*\;=\;w,
\]
where $\int_0^{a_0}G\ge\beta a_0$ by (ii). Equality is forced
throughout; we exhibit a strict inequality in every case:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $a_0>0$: (ii) is pointwise strict on $[0,a_0)$, so
$\int_0^{a_0}G>\beta a_0$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G\ne\beta$ on a positive-measure subset of
$(0,u^*)$: there $\tilde k(G)>\beta$ strictly
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}), so $\int_0^{u^*}\tilde k(G)>\beta u^*$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G=\beta$ a.e.\ on $(0,u^*)$: for each
$b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ choose $a'<\bar a(b)$ with $G(a')=\beta$
(possible, as such $a'$ have full measure in a nonempty interval);
the \emph{strict} inequality \eqref{eq:H} at the admissible corner
$(a',b)$ reads $(1-\beta)(1+\beta-2G(b))<\gamma^2=(1-\beta)(1+\beta)$,
forcing $G(b)>0$ for every $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$; hence
$\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>0$ and $w\ge\int_0^{u^*}G+
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>\beta u^*=w$.
\end{itemize}
Each case contradicts $w=w$; so \eqref{eq:H} is impossible, proving
\eqref{eq:wedge} and the coverage statement.

\emph{Sharpness.} Two-point distributions $F=(1-q)\delta_0+q\delta_u$
give failure probability $q^2$ when $q<\beta$ (the pair $(u,u)$, since
$\beta u> qu=w$) and additionally the mixed pairs when $q<B$
(since $Bu>qu$), totalling $1-(1-q)^2$; letting $q\uparrow\beta$ and
$q\uparrow B$ respectively yields failure probabilities approaching
$\beta^2=\alpha$ and $1-\gamma^2=\alpha$. Hence $\sup_F\pr(\SB<\mu)
=\alpha$.

\emph{Non-attainment.} If some $F$ attained it, the weak form of
\eqref{eq:H} would hold, and rerunning the budget with weak
inequalities forces $G=\beta$ a.e.\ on $(0,u^*)$ (by strictness of
$\tilde k$ off $\beta$) and $G=0$ a.e.\ beyond $u^*$; right-continuous
monotonicity then pins $F=(1-\beta)\delta_0+\beta\delta_{u^*}$, whose
largest value of $Am+BM$ is $\beta u^*=w$ exactly, so
$\pr(Am+BM>w)=0\ne\alpha$ --- a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to known inequalities]\label{rem:known}
The equal-weights case of \eqref{eq:wedge} --- bounding
$\pr(X_1+X_2\ge t)$ for nonnegative i.i.d.\ variables with given
mean --- is the classical sharp inequality of Hoeffding and Shrikhande
\cite{hs1955} (extended to general $k$ in \cite{lms2016}); the wedge
inequality is its order-statistic-weighted analogue, with genuinely
unequal weights $A\ne B$ for all $\alpha\in(0,1)$. The sharp-bound
literature for order statistics and L-statistics under moment
conditions (see \cite{balrych2024}) concerns expectations, or
distribution functions of single order statistics, not
mean-constrained tail probabilities of weighted combinations; we are
not aware of a prior result implying \eqref{eq:wedge}. Separately, the
valid-mean-bound literature offers an instructive comparison: Gaffke's
bound \cite{gaffke2005}, whose finite-sample validity for independent
variables was proved only recently \cite{vlassis2026}, is pointwise
dominated by the $n=2$ Stringer bound at $\alpha=0.05$ (numerically:
minimum gap $0$, attained exactly on the two extremal families ---
equal taints, and one taint equal to $1$), so at standard confidence
levels conservatism at $n=2$ would alternatively follow from
\cite{vlassis2026} \emph{given} a proof of that domination; but the
domination fails at $\alpha>1/2$ (e.g.\ by $-0.032$ at $\alpha=0.7$),
where Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} still holds, so the present proof is
strictly stronger and self-contained. Anderson's bound
\cite{anderson1969} does not dominate or get dominated similarly.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Second proof]\label{rem:second}
An independent verification produced a structurally different proof of
\eqref{eq:wedge}: decompose the failure probability as
$g^2+2\int_{[0,u^*)}G(h(u))\,dF(u)$ with $g=G(u^*)$, bound the tail
survival by mass counting, majorize linearly in the high-part mean, and
check two endpoints. Its closing inequality,
$J(g)=A(\beta+g)-2B(1-g)\ge0$ on $[B,\beta]$ with $J(B)=\beta^2+
\gamma^2-1=0$, is algebraically equivalent to Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}. Two
different arguments hinging on the same tight inequality, with the same
equality points $g\in\{B,\beta\}$ matching the two extremal families,
corroborate the sharpness structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}
For $n=2$ the Stringer bound with Poisson (AICPA) factors is
conservative for every $F$ and every $\alpha$ for which the Poisson
factors dominate the binomial ones; the domination is verified with
rigorous enclosures for standard levels in the repository.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Single nonzero taint value]\label{prop:twopoint}
For every $n$, $\alpha$, and $F$ supported on $\{0,v\}$ with
$v\in(0,1]$: $\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $K$ the number of nonzero taints, $K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)$,
$\SB=(1-v)p_n(0)+v\,p_n(K)\ge v\,p_n(K)$ and $\mu=qv$; on the
Clopper--Pearson coverage event $\{p_n(K)\ge q\}$, of probability at
least $1-\alpha$, we get $\SB\ge vq=\mu$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact counterexamples at low confidence}\label{sec:counter}

Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} covers every confidence level, so the asymptotic
anti-conservatism at $\alpha>1/2$ \cite{papvz1996} must manifest only at
larger $n$. We locate it precisely. All claims below are certified: the
taint distribution's parameters are exact dyadic rationals, coverage is
computed as an exact rational by full multinomial enumeration, and a
margin certificate verifies that no comparison $\SB\ge\mu$ was decided
within the numerical error of the confidence factors (which are enclosed
in bisection brackets of width $10^{-40}$).

\end{table}

A representative certificate: at nominal confidence $30\%$
($\alpha=0.7$), $n=50$, taints $v_1\approx0.99$, $v_2\approx0.4655$
with probabilities $q_1\approx0.246$, $q_2\approx0.592$ (exact dyadic
values in the repository), the exact coverage is
$0.29888\ldots<0.30$, with minimum margin $\min|\SB-\mu|\approx
2\times10^{-17}$ against a factor-error bound of $5\times10^{-39}$.
These are, to our knowledge, the first exact counterexamples at
explicit parameters in the literature; \cite{papvz1995} established
existence for uniform-type taints via recursions but the present
examples are two-atom, elementary to re-verify, and map the failure
boundary.

At and above $50\%$ confidence the picture reverses. Grid-plus-optimizer
searches over two-atom populations ($n\le100$ at $95\%$ and $90\%$;
$n\le300$ at $50\%$) and three-atom populations ($n\le25$ at $95\%$)
find \emph{no} violation; at every $n$ the screened infimum equals the
nominal level to $10^{-10}$ or better, approached as the top taint
$v_1\to1$ --- consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:twopoint} and with
the $\{0,1\}$-support minimality of \cite{djpvz1997}, and evidence that
the conjecture, if true, is exactly tight. For the Poisson factors used
in practice the worst-case coverage stays strictly above nominal, e.g.\
at $95\%$: $0.9716$ ($n=10$), $0.9573$ ($n=50$), $0.9549$ ($n=100$) ---
a finite-sample cushion that erodes as $n$ grows.

\section{The gap in the claimed certification}\label{sec:bimpeh}

Bimpeh \cite{bimpeh2008} writes the Stringer bound as
$\SB=\int_0^1[1-\widehat F_{n,L}(t)]\,dt$, where $\widehat F_{n,L}$ is
the stepwise band of lower Clopper--Pearson limits $q_n(i-1)$ on
$[t_{i-1:n},t_{i:n})$, and derives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bimpeh}
\mathrm{CP}\;\ge\;\bar P_n\;=\;\pr\bigl(U_{i:n}\le p_n(i),\ 1\le i\le
n\bigr),
\end{equation}
computing $\bar P_n$ by Bolshev's recursion and concluding that
\eqref{eq:conj} holds for all $F$ when $\bar P_n\ge1-\alpha$ --- e.g.\
$n\le11$ at $\alpha=0.05$ --- and, since
$\bar P_2 = 2\sqrt{1-\alpha}-(1-\alpha)\ge1-\alpha$ for all $\alpha$,
claiming in particular a proof of \eqref{eq:conj} for $n\le2$. This is
the only finite-sample certification claimed anywhere in the
literature; to our knowledge it has never been cited critically, so
the error identified below has gone unnoticed since 2008.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bimpehwrong}
Inequality \eqref{eq:bimpeh} is false, for continuous and for atomic
$F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha=0.05$ and let $F$ put mass $0.99$ uniformly on
$(0.99,1)$ and $0.01$ uniformly on $(0,0.01)$, so $\mu=0.9851$. For
$n=1$: $\SB=0.95+0.05\,T_1$ and $\bar P_1=\pr(U\le p_1(1))=1$; but
$\SB<\mu\iff T_1<0.702$, an event of probability exactly $0.01$, so
$\mathrm{CP}=0.99<1=\bar P_1$. For $n=2$: $\bar
P_2=2\sqrt{0.95}-0.95=0.99936$, while the pair (one high taint, one
low) fails, giving $\mathrm{CP}=1-2(0.99)(0.01)-0.01^2=0.9801<\bar
P_2$. An atomic example at $n=5$: $F$ with
$\pr(T=1)=\tfrac12$, $\pr(T=\tfrac1{10})=\pr(T=0)=\tfrac14$ has exact
coverage $\tfrac{31}{32}=0.96875<\bar P_5=0.98746$ (the failure event
is precisely the $2^{-5}$-probability event of drawing no taint-$1$
unit).
\end{proof}

The error is an index shift: pointwise domination $F\ge\widehat
F_{n,L}$ on $[0,1]$ is equivalent to $F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)$ for all
$i$ --- the band's value \emph{at} the jump points --- whereas
\cite{bimpeh2008} substitutes the left limits $q_n(i-1)$, dropping in
particular the top constraint $F(t_{n:n})\ge q_n(n)=\alpha^{1/n}$.
Moreover the method cannot be repaired:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cannot}
For every continuous $F$ and every $n$, the corrected containment
probability satisfies
$\pr\bigl(F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)
=\pr\bigl(U_{i:n}\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)\le
\pr\bigl(U_{n:n}\ge\alpha^{1/n}\bigr)=1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Thus the pointwise-band route yields at most $1-\alpha$ and
can certify conservatism at no $n$: if \eqref{eq:conj} is true, it is
true because the integral inequality $\int\widehat F_{n,L}\le\int F$
holds on a strictly larger event than pointwise domination. (The values
of $\bar P_n$ themselves, and Bimpeh's Table 5.1, reproduce exactly;
they are simply not coverage bounds.) In particular the $n=1,2$
counterexamples of Proposition~\ref{prop:bimpehwrong} refute
\eqref{eq:bimpeh} at precisely the sample sizes for which
\cite{bimpeh2008} claimed an analytic proof, so that claim is invalid
and Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} is, to our knowledge, the first correct
finite-sample proof at $n=2$. The proven record therefore reduces to
$n=1$ \cite{bickel1992}, $\{0,1\}$ supports \cite{djpvz1997},
single-value supports (Proposition~\ref{prop:twopoint}), and now $n=2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:n2}).

\section{Discussion}

Three observations orient future work. First, the reformulation behind
\eqref{eq:red} generalizes: with $u_{(1)}\le\cdots\le u_{(n)}$ the
increasing order statistics of $U_i=1-T_i$,
\[
\SB\;=\;1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_j\,u_{(j)},\qquad
e_j=p_n(j)-p_n(j-1),
\]
so \eqref{eq:conj} is a family of weighted order-statistic exceedance
inequalities, with all audit content absorbed into the decreasing
weights $e_j$. Second, the $n=2$ proof's architecture --- rectangle
lower bounds on the covering event, a survival-integral budget, and a
concave potential with equal endpoint values --- does not obviously
depend on $n=2$ except through the explicit factor algebra; the
$n\ge3$ factors lack closed forms, so the endpoint identities
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}) would need structural rather than algebraic
proofs. Third, the empirical sharpness at every $n$ and the certified
failures below $50\%$ confidence suggest the true statement is exactly
\eqref{eq:conj} for $\alpha\le1/2$, with the infimum equal to
$1-\alpha$ at every $n$ --- a conjecture now precise enough to attack.

For practice: the binomial Stringer bound has no worst-case safety
margin at all ($95\%$ means exactly $95\%$ against adversarial
populations), the Poisson tables retain a quantifiable but eroding
cushion, and evaluations below $50\%$ confidence are provably
anti-conservative at audit-relevant sample sizes.
