\begin{abstract}
The Stringer bound is widely used to evaluate monetary-unit samples in
auditing, but its finite-sample coverage is not known in general. For sample
size $n=2$, we prove that the binomial-factor bound is conservative for every
distribution on $[0,1]$ and every nominal confidence level. The result is
sharp: the supremum of noncoverage is $\alpha$ and is not attained. At
$90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ confidence, exact Bernstein certificates extend
binomial-factor conservatism through $n=6$. For the Poisson factors used in
audit practice, a corrected simultaneous-survival-band argument and exact
rational certificates establish conservatism through $n=8$, $n=11$, and
$n=20$, respectively. For every sample size and confidence level, scalar
calibration of the Poisson factor curve yields a distribution-free valid
modified rule. At nominal confidence at least $2/e\approx73.6\%$, the
multiplier two is valid uniformly in the sample size; sharper uniform choices
are $1.76$, $1.66$, and $1.52$ at $90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$, respectively.
An anchored variant leaves the all-zero-sample result unchanged. A separate all-sample-size rule
reports the maximum of Stringer and a distribution-free bounded-mean limit.
We also give explicit
finite-sample counterexamples at selected confidence levels between $30\%$
and $37\%$ and sample sizes $n=50,100,200,400$; exact rational certificates
show coverage below nominal. In searches at $50\%$, $90\%$, and $95\%$, no
violation was found, and the smallest coverage found agreed with nominal to
within $10^{-10}$. Finally, we identify an index shift in a previous
finite-sample certification. These results give sharp distribution-free
theory at $n=2$, prove conventional-level coverage over new finite ranges,
provide valid all-sample-size reporting alternatives, and narrow the
unresolved regime for ordinary Stringer.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Stringer bound; finite-sample coverage;
bounded means; monetary-unit sampling; Poisson confidence factors;
simultaneous bands; Clopper--Pearson limits.

\section{Introduction}

Monetary-unit sampling (MUS) draws $n$ dollar units at random from an
audited population. The \emph{taint} of a drawn unit is $T\in[0,1]$,
the overstated fraction of the account item associated with that dollar.
With taints $t_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge t_{(n)}$ arranged in nonincreasing
order, the Stringer bound \cite{stringer1963} at nominal confidence
$1-\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sb}
\SB \;=\; p_n(0) \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(p_n(j)-p_n(j-1)\bigr)\,t_{(j)},
\end{equation}
where $p_n(j)$ is the upper Clopper--Pearson limit for $j$ successes in
$n$ trials, i.e.\ the root of $\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{n}{i}p^i(1-p)^{n-i}
=\alpha$, with $p_n(n)=1$. Audit guidance also tabulates Poisson-based
confidence factors for MUS evaluation \cite[Appendix~C]{aicpa2012}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} below proves that these factors
dominate the binomial factors, at every sample size, whenever nominal
confidence exceeds $1-e^{-1}\approx63.2\%$. The method is commonly
justified by the conjecture that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
\pr\bigl(\SB \ge \mu(F)\bigr) \;\ge\; 1-\alpha
\qquad\text{for all } n \text{ and all } F \text{ on } [0,1],
\end{equation}
where $T_1,\dots,T_n$ are drawn independently from the common
distribution $F$, with mean $\mu(F)$. This is the standard i.i.d.\ model
used in published analyses of the bound. A National Research Council
panel noted that the formulation of the Stringer bound had not been
satisfactorily explained \cite{panel1989}. The principal prior results
are as follows. Bickel \cite{bickel1992} proved
\eqref{eq:conj} for $n=1$, gave the lower bound $(1-\alpha)^{n+1}$ for
$n\ge2$ under conditions on $F$, and derived the expansion
$\SB = \bar T + c(F)z_{1-\alpha}n^{-1/2}+o_p(n^{-1/2})$ with
$c^2(F)\ge\operatorname{Var}(T)$. Pap and van Zuijlen
\cite{papvz1996} established an almost-sure version of this expansion
and concluded that the
bound is asymptotically conservative for $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12]$ and
asymptotically \emph{anti}-conservative for $\alpha\in(\tfrac12,1)$.
In \cite{papvz1995}, they exhibited non-conservatism for uniform-type
taints at sub-$50\%$ confidence using exact recursions. De Jager, Pap,
and van Zuijlen \cite{djpvz1997} proved that, for taint distributions
supported on $\{0,1\}$, the
bound reduces to Clopper--Pearson (where \eqref{eq:conj} holds exactly)
and that Stringer's coefficients are minimal with that property. A 2021
survey of confidence bounds for bounded means categorized the Stringer
bound among methods conjectured to have guaranteed coverage and
described its coverage for $\alpha<0.5$ as unknown
\cite[p.~8569]{phan2021}.
Vlassis and Thomas \cite{vlassis2026} recently proved the finite-sample
validity of Gaffke's distribution-free mean test \cite{gaffke2005}.
Inversion for observations in $[0,1]$ gives an upper confidence limit that
is a quantile of a uniform Dirichlet average
\cite{learnedmiller2020,ming2026}. This development supplies the valid
comparison bound used for our $n=3$ through $n=6$ results.

This paper makes five contributions.

\emph{(1) A theorem at $n=2$} (Section~\ref{sec:thm}). The conjecture
\eqref{eq:conj} holds at $n=2$ for every $F$ and every
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, including $\alpha>1/2$. Thus the known asymptotic
failures do not occur at $n=2$, and the result is sharp. To our
knowledge, this is the first distribution-free finite-sample proof for
$n=2$.

\emph{(2) Conventional confidence levels from $n=3$ through $n=6$}
(Section~\ref{sec:n3}). At nominal confidence $90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$,
the binomial Stringer bound pointwise dominates the valid Gaffke upper
limit. The comparison is proved by exact rational Bernstein certificates
for the relevant uniform-simplex cap volumes. It yields distribution-free
finite-sample conservatism at each of these levels for $n=3,4,5,6$. A
directed-interval Bernstein certificate handles the five-dimensional case.
The same comparison suggests a direct all-sample-size implementation
safeguard (Section~\ref{sec:safeguard}): pre-specifying the maximum of
Stringer and the valid Gaffke limit
retains Stringer whenever it is larger and has distribution-free coverage
without assuming the unresolved conjecture. A dimension-free cap lemma and
an analytic Clopper--Pearson comparison further prove a directly checkable
zero-uplift region for every $n$ whenever nominal confidence is at least
$75\%$: binomial Stringer dominates Gaffke whenever it is
at least the largest observed taint.

\emph{(3) Guarantees for the factors used in practice}
(Section~\ref{sec:poisson}). Whenever nominal confidence exceeds
$1-e^{-1}\approx63.2\%$, the Poisson factors dominate the binomial
factors coordinatewise for every $n$. A summation-by-parts identity then
shows that the Poisson-factor Stringer bound is pointwise no smaller on
every observed sample. A separate corrected simultaneous-band argument,
certified by exact rational boundary-crossing calculations, proves
Poisson-factor coverage for every $n\le8$ at $90\%$, every $n\le11$ at
$95\%$, and every $n\le20$ at $99\%$ confidence. The same argument yields
an all-sample-size calibrated rule that retains the shape of the ordinary
Poisson factor curve: multiply every factor, equivalently the complete bound, by one
precomputed scalar. We prove validity for every $n$ and give exact rational
certificates for representative audit sample sizes. An anchored variant
fixes the zero-taint factor and scales only the error increments. Capping
the calibrated factors at one gives pointwise no-larger valid versions. A
sample-size-uniform analytic bound shows that doubling the Poisson curve is
valid whenever nominal confidence is at least $2/e\approx73.6\%$; at the
three conventional levels, smaller simple choices are certified.

\emph{(4) Exact counterexamples at low confidence}
(Section~\ref{sec:counter}). Thirty-three certified three-point
distributions, each with two nonzero taint values and an atom at zero,
give counterexamples at selected nominal confidence levels between
$30\%$ and $37\%$, for sample sizes $n=50,100,200,$ and $400$. Their
coverage is evaluated in exact rational arithmetic with margin
certificates. In searches at $50\%$, $90\%$, and $95\%$, no violation
was found. At every sample size searched, the smallest coverage found
agreed with the nominal level to within $10^{-10}$.

\emph{(5) Reassessment of a previous certification}
(Section~\ref{sec:bimpeh}). A proposed finite-sample certification
\cite{bimpeh2008} contains an index shift in its band-containment
argument. We give hand-checkable examples for which the stated
containment inequality fails and examine the corrected event.

\section{Finite-sample conservatism at \texorpdfstring{$n=2$}{n=2}}\label{sec:thm}

For $n=2$, the factors are explicit: $p_2(0)=1-\sqrt{\alpha}$,
$p_2(1)=\sqrt{1-\alpha}$, $p_2(2)=1$. Write
\[
\beta=\sqrt{\alpha},\quad \gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\quad(\beta^2+\gamma^2=1),
\qquad A = \beta+\gamma-1,\quad B=1-\gamma,
\]
so $A,B>0$ and $A+B=\beta$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n2}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and let $T_1,T_2$ be i.i.d.\ with common
distribution $F$ on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. Then
$\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge 1-\alpha$. Moreover
$\sup_F \pr(\SB<\mu)=\alpha$, and the supremum is not attained.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Reduction}

Substituting $U_i=1-T_i$, $w=1-\mu=\E[U]$, $m=\min(U_1,U_2)$,
$M=\max(U_1,U_2)$ into \eqref{eq:sb} and telescoping gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:red}
\SB \;=\; 1-\bigl(A\,m+B\,M\bigr),
\end{equation}
so $\SB\ge\mu\iff Am+BM\le w$. The theorem is implied by the
\emph{wedge inequality}: for $U_1,U_2$ i.i.d.\ on $[0,1]$ with mean
$w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge}
\pr\bigl(Am+BM< w\bigr)\;\ge\;\gamma^2.
\end{equation}
(If $w=0$ then $U\equiv0$ a.s.\ and $\SB=1\ge\mu$ almost surely.)

If $w>\beta$ the event $\{Am+BM\ge w\}$ is empty, since the left side
is at most $A+B=\beta$; if $w=\beta$ it is exactly $\{U_1=U_2=1\}$
(as $A,B>0$), of probability $\pr(U=1)^2\le w^2=\alpha$ by Markov's
inequality.

Henceforth, assume $0<w<\beta$. The proof has three steps. First, the
rectangle lemma converts a hypothetical failure of the wedge inequality
into pointwise lower bounds on the survival function $G$. Second,
boundary rectangles relate values of $G$ on opposite sides of $u^*$.
Third, the potential inequality combines those bounds and forces the
integral of $G$ to exceed its prescribed value $w$, producing a
contradiction.

\subsection{Rectangle bounds}

Let $F(x)=\pr(U\le x)$, $G=1-F$, and recall $w=\int_0^1G$. Define
\[
u^*=\frac{w}{\beta}\in(0,1),\qquad
a_0=\max\Bigl(0,\frac{w-B}{A}\Bigr),\qquad
T^\dagger=\min\Bigl(\frac{w}{B},1\Bigr),
\]
and note $a_0<u^*<T^\dagger$.

\begin{lemma}[Rectangle lemma]\label{lem:rect}
If $0\le a\le b\le 1$ and $Aa+Bb<w$, then
$\pr(Am+BM<w)\ge 2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $\{U_1\le a,U_2\le b\}\cup\{U_1\le b,U_2\le a\}$ we have $m\le a$,
$M\le b$, hence $Am+BM\le Aa+Bb<w$ (using $A,B\ge0$). By
inclusion--exclusion (the intersection is $\{U_1\le a, U_2\le a\}$
since $a\le b$) the union has probability $2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2$.
\end{proof}

Assume for contradiction that \eqref{eq:wedge} fails. Then
\begin{equation*}\label{eq:H}
\tag{H}
2F(a)F(b)-F(a)^2<\gamma^2
\quad\text{whenever } 0\le a\le b\le 1,\; Aa+Bb<w.
\end{equation*}
Three families of admissible rectangles give pointwise bounds on $G$:

\emph{(i) Squares.} For $t<u^*$, $(a,b)=(t,t)$ gives $F(t)^2<\gamma^2$,
i.e.\ $G(t)>1-\gamma=B$ for all $t\in[0,u^*)$.

\emph{(ii) Full-height rectangles} (nonvacuous if and only if $w>B$).
For $a<a_0$,
$(a,1)$ gives $1-G(a)^2<\gamma^2$, i.e.\ $G(a)>\beta$ on $[0,a_0)$.

\emph{(iii) Boundary pairs.} Let $h(g)=\dfrac{\beta^2-g^2}{2(1-g)}$ on
$[0,1)$. For $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ set $\bar a(b)=(w-Bb)/A\in
(a_0,u^*)$. Every $a'<\bar a(b)$ is admissible with $b$; letting
$a'\uparrow\bar a(b)$ and writing $g=G(\bar a(b)^-)<1$,
inequality \eqref{eq:H} rearranges to
\[
G(b)\;\ge\;h\bigl(G(\bar a(b)^-)\bigr)
\qquad\text{for all } b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)
\]
because $2(1-g)-\gamma^2-(1-g)^2=\beta^2-g^2$. If
$g=1$ the bound $G(b)\ge0$ is used instead, consistent with the
convention $h_+(1)=0$ below.

\subsection{Potential function and budget}

The function $\tilde k$ below is chosen to combine the contribution of
$G(a)$ with the corresponding lower bound for $G(b(a))$ after the
change of variables used below. Let $h_+=\max(h,0)$, extended
continuously by $h_+(1)=0$, and define
\[
\tilde k(g)\;=\;g+\frac{A}{B}\,h_+(g),\qquad g\in[B,1].
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pot}
$\tilde k(g)\ge\beta$ on $[B,1]$, with equality exactly when
$g\in\{B,\beta\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\beta^2-B^2=\alpha-(1-\gamma)^2=2\gamma(1-\gamma)$, we get
$h(B)=B$, hence $\tilde k(B)=B+\frac{A}{B}B=A+B=\beta$; also
$h(\beta)=0$ so $\tilde k(\beta)=\beta$. Differentiating,
$h'(g)=\frac{(1-g)^2-\gamma^2}{2(1-g)^2}$, so $h$ has its unique
critical point at $g=B$ (where $(1-g)^2=\gamma^2$) and decreases on
$(B,1)$; hence $h\ge0$ on $[B,\beta]$ (it decreases from $B>0$ to $0$)
and $h<0$ on $(\beta,1)$. Further
$h''(g)=-\gamma^2/(1-g)^3<0$: $h$, and therefore
$\tilde k=g+\frac{A}{B}h$, is strictly concave on $[B,\beta]$. A
strictly concave function with equal endpoint values $\beta$ satisfies
$\tilde k\ge\beta$ on $[B,\beta]$, with equality only at the endpoints.
On
$(\beta,1]$, $h_+=0$ and $\tilde k(g)=g>\beta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:n2}]
Assume \eqref{eq:H}. The substitution $t=b(a)=(w-Aa)/B$ is a
decreasing bijection from $[a_0,u^*]$ onto $[u^*,T^\dagger]$, with
$|dt|=(A/B)\,da$ (endpoints: $b(u^*)=u^*$; $b(a_0)=1=T^\dagger$ if
$w\ge B$, $b(0)=w/B=T^\dagger$ if $w\le B$). Using
$w=\int_0^1G\ge\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G+\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G$,
we bound the tail with (iii) (the left-limit $G(a^-)$ agrees with $G(a)$
for all but countably many $a$, so the integrals coincide):
\[
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G
=\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}G(b(a))\,da
\;\ge\;\frac{A}{B}\int_{a_0}^{u^*}h_+\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da.
\]
By (i), $G>B$ pointwise on $[a_0,u^*)$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:pot} applies
under the integral:
\[
w\;\ge\;\int_0^{a_0}G+\int_{a_0}^{u^*}\tilde k\bigl(G(a)\bigr)\,da
\;\ge\;\beta a_0+\beta(u^*-a_0)\;=\;\beta u^*\;=\;w,
\]
where $\int_0^{a_0}G\ge\beta a_0$ by (ii). Equality is forced
throughout; we exhibit a strict inequality in every case:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $a_0>0$: (ii) is pointwise strict on $[0,a_0)$, so
$\int_0^{a_0}G>\beta a_0$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G\ne\beta$ on a positive-measure subset of
$(0,u^*)$: since $G>B$ there, $\tilde k(G)>\beta$ strictly
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pot}), so $\int_0^{u^*}\tilde k(G)>\beta u^*$.
\item If $a_0=0$ and $G=\beta$ a.e.\ on $(0,u^*)$: for each
$b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$ choose $a'<\bar a(b)$ with $G(a')=\beta$
(possible, as such $a'$ have full measure in a nonempty interval);
the \emph{strict} inequality \eqref{eq:H} at the admissible corner
$(a',b)$ reads $(1-\beta)(1+\beta-2G(b))<\gamma^2=(1-\beta)(1+\beta)$,
forcing $G(b)>0$ for every $b\in(u^*,T^\dagger)$; hence
$\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>0$ and $w\ge\int_0^{u^*}G+
\int_{u^*}^{T^\dagger}G>\beta u^*=w$.
\end{itemize}
Each case yields $w>w$, a contradiction. Thus \eqref{eq:H} is
impossible, proving
\eqref{eq:wedge} and the coverage statement.

\emph{Sharpness.} Distributions supported on $\{0,u\}$, with
$F=(1-q)\delta_0+q\delta_u$, give failure probability $q^2$ when
$q<\beta$ (the pair $(u,u)$, since
$\beta u> qu=w$) and additionally the mixed pairs when $q<B$
(since $Bu>qu$), totalling $1-(1-q)^2$; letting $q\uparrow\beta$ and
$q\uparrow B$ respectively yields failure probabilities approaching
$\beta^2=\alpha$ and $1-\gamma^2=\alpha$. Hence $\sup_F\pr(\SB<\mu)
=\alpha$.

\emph{Non-attainment.} If some distribution $F$ attained the supremum,
then $\pr(Am+BM>w)=\alpha$. Such a distribution must have
$0<w<\beta$, since the strict failure event is empty in the boundary
regimes handled above. The coverage result just proved gives
$\pr(Am+BM<w)\ge\gamma^2$; since $\alpha+\gamma^2=1$, attainment would
therefore require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:attainzero}
\pr(Am+BM<w)=\gamma^2,
\qquad \pr(Am+BM=w)=0.
\end{equation}
The rectangle bounds consequently hold with weak inequalities. Repeating
the budget argument in that form forces equality at every step. Equality
on the first interval gives $G=\beta$ almost everywhere on $(0,a_0)$,
and equality in the potential bound permits
$G\in\{B,\beta\}$ almost everywhere on $(a_0,u^*)$. Because $G$ is
nonincreasing, there is a $c\in[a_0,u^*]$ such that its almost-everywhere
level is $\beta$ before $c$ and $B$ after $c$. Set
$d=b(c)=(w-Ac)/B$. Equality in the tail comparison, together with
$h_+(\beta)=0$ and $h_+(B)=B$, gives $G(b(a))=0$ for almost every
$a\in(a_0,c)$ and $G(b(a))=B$ for almost every $a\in(c,u^*)$.
Because $b$ is decreasing, this is the corresponding switch at $d$ on
the other side of $u^*$. Equality in the discarded tail and
right-continuity then force
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equalitysurvival}
G(x)=
\begin{cases}
\beta, & 0\le x<c,\\
B, & c\le x<d,\\
0, & d\le x\le1,
\end{cases}
\qquad Ac+Bd=w.
\end{equation}
The change of variables gives $c\le u^*\le d$. Every distribution in
\eqref{eq:equalitysurvival} places positive probability on the boundary
$Am+BM=w$. If $0<c<d$, it has masses $A$ and $B$ at $c$ and $d$, so
the mixed pair $(c,d)$ has probability $2AB>0$. If $c=0<d$, the atoms
at $0$ and $d$ have masses $1-B$ and $B$, so their mixed pair has
positive probability. The remaining case $c=d$ forces $c=d=u^*$, and
the pair $(u^*,u^*)$ has probability $\beta^2>0$. Each case contradicts
\eqref{eq:attainzero}. Hence the supremum is not attained.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to known inequalities]\label{rem:known}
The equal-weights case of \eqref{eq:wedge}, which bounds
$\pr(X_1+X_2\ge t)$ for nonnegative i.i.d.\ variables with a given
mean, is the classical sharp inequality of Hoeffding and Shrikhande
\cite{hs1955} (extended to general $k$ in \cite{lms2016}); the wedge
inequality is its order-statistic-weighted analogue, with genuinely
unequal weights except at $\alpha=16/25$, where $A=B$. The sharp-bound
literature for order statistics and L-statistics under moment
conditions (see \cite{balrych2024}) concerns expectations or
distribution functions of single order statistics, not
mean-constrained tail probabilities of weighted combinations; we are
not aware of a prior result implying \eqref{eq:wedge}. Other
distribution-free upper bounds for bounded means include those of
Anderson \cite{anderson1969} and Gaffke \cite{gaffke2005}; the latter's
finite-sample validity for independent variables was proved recently
\cite{vlassis2026}. Those results are not used here, and the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} is self-contained.
\end{remark}

\section{Conventional confidence levels from
\texorpdfstring{$n=3$ through $n=6$}{n=3 through n=6}}\label{sec:n3}

The recent validity proof for Gaffke's mean test \cite{vlassis2026}
provides a second route to finite-sample coverage. For a real random
variable $X$, write $Q_q(X)=\inf\{z:\Pr(X\le z)\ge q\}$. Given $n$
observed taints $t_1,\ldots,t_n$, define
\[
G_\alpha(t_1,\ldots,t_n)
=Q_{1-\alpha}\!\left(D_0+\sum_{i=1}^nt_iD_i\right),
\qquad (D_0,\ldots,D_n)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1).
\]
This is the one-sided Gaffke--Learned-Miller--Thomas upper limit
\cite{gaffke2005,learnedmiller2020,ming2026}; it has coverage at least
$1-\alpha$ for independent observations from every distribution on
$[0,1]$. We prove that Stringer is pointwise no smaller at three
conventional confidence levels for $n=3$, $n=4$, $n=5$, and $n=6$.

\subsection{The case \texorpdfstring{$n=3$}{n=3}}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n3}
Let $n=3$ and let $T_1,T_2,T_3$ be i.i.d. with common distribution $F$
on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. If
$\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$, then
\[
\SB\ge G_\alpha(T_1,T_2,T_3)
\quad\text{for every observed sample},
\qquad
\Pr_F(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the binomial Stringer bound is distribution-free conservative at
$90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ nominal confidence when $n=3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
a=1-p_3(2),\quad b=p_3(2)-p_3(1),\quad
c=p_3(1)-p_3(0),\quad d=p_3(0).
\]
For ascending sample taints $t_1\le t_2\le t_3$,
$\SB=at_1+bt_2+ct_3+d$. The four coefficients are positive and sum to
one. Unless $t_1=1$, affine equivariance permits subtraction of $t_1$
and division by $1-t_1$. It therefore suffices to use the four knots
$(0,x,y,1)$, where $0\le x\le y\le1$, and the Stringer threshold
\[
s=bx+cy+d.
\]
By exchangeability of the Dirichlet weights, pointwise domination of
$G_\alpha$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n3capgoal}
V(x,y):=\Pr_D\{xD_1+yD_2+D_3>s\}\le\alpha.
\end{equation}
The uniform-simplex cap formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n3caps}
V(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(1-s)^3}{(1-x)(1-y)},&s\ge y,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{1-y}\left\{
\dfrac{(1-s)^3}{1-x}-\dfrac{(y-s)^3}{y(y-x)}\right\},
&x\le s\le y,\\[8pt]
1-\dfrac{s^3}{xy},&s\le x,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with boundary values defined by continuity. The Clopper--Pearson
equations imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n3identities}
(a+b+c)^3=\alpha,\qquad (b+c+d)^3=1-\alpha,\qquad
(a+b)^2\{3-2(a+b)\}=\alpha.
\end{equation}

We verify \eqref{eq:n3capgoal} in the three regions of
\eqref{eq:n3caps}. If $s\le x$, put $g=b+c+d=(1-\alpha)^{1/3}$.
The exact factor enclosures certify $b,c\le g/3$ at each of the three
values of $\alpha$. Weighted AM--GM therefore gives
\[
\frac{s}{g}=\frac bgx+\frac cgy+\frac dg
\ge x^{b/g}y^{c/g}\ge(xy)^{1/3},
\]
and the third line of \eqref{eq:n3caps} is at most $\alpha$.

For $s\ge y$, set $X=1-x$, $Y=1-y$, $L=1-s=a+bX+cY$, and
$A=1-b-c=a+d$. The domain is the triangle with vertices
\[
\left(\frac aA,\frac aA\right),\qquad (1,1),\qquad
\left(1,\frac{a+b}{1-c}\right).
\]
On that triangle the desired inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n3regiona}
P_A(X,Y):=\alpha XY-(a+bX+cY)^3\ge0.
\end{equation}
After an affine map from the standard triangle, all degree-three
Bernstein coefficients of $P_A$ are positive except the coefficient at
$(X,Y)=(1,1)$, which is zero by the first identity in
\eqref{eq:n3identities}. The coefficient signs are certified by exact
rational intervals as described below.

It remains to consider $x\le s\le y$. Write
$\rho=s-x\ge0$ and $\tau=y-s\ge0$. Solving for $x,y$ shows that the
$(\rho,\tau)$ domain is the quadrilateral with vertices
\[
(0,0),\quad\left(\frac d{1-c},0\right),\quad
(c+d,a+b),\quad\left(0,\frac a{1-b}\right).
\]
Triangulate it along the diagonal from $(0,0)$ to $(c+d,a+b)$.
Differentiating the middle line of \eqref{eq:n3caps} and cancelling common
factors gives
\[
\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}
=\frac{Q(x,y)}{y^2(1-x)(y-x)^2},
\]
where $Q$ is a degree-five polynomial. On both triangles, every
degree-five Bernstein coefficient of $A^3Q$ is nonnegative. Three on each
triangle are structural zeros, and all remaining coefficients are
strictly positive. Hence $V$ is nondecreasing in $y$ at fixed $x$.
Increasing $y$ preserves both middle-region inequalities, so it is enough
to take the limit $y\uparrow1$.

At $y=1$, put $q=1-x$. The constraint is
$q\ge q_0:=a/(1-b)$, and direct simplification yields
\[
V(x,1)=
\frac{(a+bq)^2\{3q-(a+bq)(1+q)\}}{q^2}.
\]
After mapping $[q_0,1]$ to $[0,1]$, the degree-four Bernstein
coefficients of
\[
\alpha q^2-(a+bq)^2\{3q-(a+bq)(1+q)\}
\]
are positive except the coefficient at $q=1$, which is zero by the final
identity in \eqref{eq:n3identities}. This completes
\eqref{eq:n3capgoal}.

For completeness, Table~\ref{tab:n3certificate} reports the smallest
positive Bernstein lower bound in each exact check. The factors are
enclosed on a dyadic grid of width $2^{-120}$; endpoint signs of their
binomial CDF equations and every subsequent coefficient sign are evaluated
with integer or rational arithmetic. The symbolic derivation starts from
\eqref{eq:n3caps} and regenerates all coefficient formulas. Both the
derivation and sign certificate are reproduced by the repository commands
\texttt{make n3-formula-check} and
\texttt{make n3-certificate-check}. Thus this is an exact
computer-assisted proof, not a grid search. Finally, validity of
$G_\alpha$ gives the stated coverage inequality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The case \texorpdfstring{$n=4$}{n=4}}

The same comparison can be carried out one dimension higher. The additional
dimension changes the cap geometry substantially: after clearing boundary
factors, two degree-six polynomials must be certified on six tetrahedra.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n4}
Let $n=4$ and let $T_1,\ldots,T_4$ be i.i.d. with common distribution $F$
on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. If
$\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$, then
\[
\SB\ge G_\alpha(T_1,\ldots,T_4)
\quad\text{for every observed sample},
\qquad
\Pr_F(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the binomial Stringer bound is distribution-free conservative at
$90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ nominal confidence when $n=4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $p_j=p_4(j)$ and put
\[
a=1-p_3,\quad b=p_3-p_2,\quad c=p_2-p_1,\quad
d=p_1-p_0,\quad e=p_0.
\]
For ascending taints $t_1\le t_2\le t_3\le t_4$,
$\SB=at_1+bt_2+ct_3+dt_4+e$. The five coefficients are positive and
sum to one. As in the $n=3$ proof, affine equivariance reduces the sample
knots to
\[
(0,x,y,z,1),\qquad 0\le x\le y\le z\le1,
\qquad s=bx+cy+dz+e.
\]
It is therefore enough to prove that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n4capgoal}
V(x,y,z):=\Pr_D\{xD_1+yD_2+zD_3+D_4>s\}\le\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $(D_0,\ldots,D_4)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)$.
Put
\[
H_1=\frac{(1-s)^4}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)},\quad
H_z=\frac{(z-s)^4}{z(z-x)(z-y)(1-z)},\quad
H_y=\frac{(y-s)^4}{y(y-x)(z-y)(1-y)}.
\]
The uniform-simplex cap formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n4caps}
V=\begin{cases}
H_1,&s\ge z,\\
H_1-H_z,&y\le s\le z,\\
H_1-H_z+H_y,&x\le s\le y,\\
1-\dfrac{s^4}{xyz},&s\le x,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with boundary values defined by continuity. The factor equations give
\begin{align}
\alpha&=(1-e)^4,\nonumber\\
\alpha&=(1-d-e)^3\{1+3(d+e)\},\nonumber\\
\alpha&=(1-c-d-e)^2\{1+2(c+d+e)+3(c+d+e)^2\},\label{eq:n4identities}\\
1-\alpha&=(b+c+d+e)^4.\nonumber
\end{align}

First suppose $s\le x$ and set $g=b+c+d+e=p_3$. The exact factor
enclosures certify $b,c,d\le g/4$ at each stated value of $\alpha$.
Weighted AM--GM gives
\[
\frac{s}{g}=\frac bgx+\frac cgy+\frac dgz+\frac eg
\ge x^{b/g}y^{c/g}z^{d/g}\ge(xyz)^{1/4}.
\]
The last identity in \eqref{eq:n4identities} now makes the last line of
\eqref{eq:n4caps} at most $\alpha$.

For the other three regions, clear the positive denominators in
\eqref{eq:n4caps}. In the region $s\ge z$, the required residual is
\[
P_A=\alpha(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)-(1-s)^4.
\]
In the region $y\le s\le z$, use
\begin{align*}
P_B={}&\alpha(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)z(z-x)(z-y)\\
&-(1-s)^4z(z-x)(z-y)+(z-s)^4(1-x)(1-y).
\end{align*}
Exact polynomial division, using the second identity in
\eqref{eq:n4identities}, gives $P_B=(1-z)R_B$, where $R_B$ has degree
six. In the region $x\le s\le y$, the corresponding cleared residual is
\begin{align*}
P_C={}&\alpha(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)z(z-x)(z-y)y(y-x)\\
&-(1-s)^4z(z-x)(z-y)y(y-x)\\
&+(z-s)^4(1-x)(1-y)y(y-x)\\
&-(y-s)^4(1-x)(1-z)z(z-x).
\end{align*}
The third identity in \eqref{eq:n4identities} gives
$P_C=(1-z)(1-y)(z-y)R_C$, with $R_C$ of degree six. Every extracted
factor is nonnegative on the ordered-knot domain.

It remains to certify $P_A,R_B,R_C$. Define
\[
\begin{gathered}
r_0=(0,0,0),\quad r_1=\left(0,0,\frac e{1-d}\right),\quad
r_2=\left(0,\frac e{1-c-d},\frac e{1-c-d}\right),\\
r_3=(t,t,t),\quad t=\frac e{1-b-c-d},\qquad v_{001}=(0,0,1),\\
q_2=\left(0,\frac{d+e}{1-c},1\right),\quad
q_3=(u,u,1),\quad u=\frac{d+e}{1-b-c},\\
v_{011}=(0,1,1),\qquad
q_4=\left(\frac{c+d+e}{1-b},1,1\right).
\end{gathered}
\]
Intersecting $0\le x\le y\le z\le1$ successively with the planes
$s=z$, $s=y$, and $s=x$ gives the following tetrahedralizations:
\begin{align*}
s\ge z:\quad&[r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3],\\
y\le s\le z:\quad&[r_3,r_2,r_1,v_{001}],\ [r_3,q_2,q_3,v_{001}],\
[r_3,q_2,r_2,v_{001}],\\
x\le s\le y:\quad&[r_3,q_2,r_2,v_{011}],\ [q_3,r_3,q_4,v_{011}],\
[q_3,r_3,q_2,v_{011}].
\end{align*}
The listed tetrahedra meet only on common faces and cover the respective
convex polytopes; this also follows directly by slicing at the common
diagonals $[r_3,v_{001}]$ and $[r_3,v_{011}]$.

Map each tetrahedron affinely to the standard tetrahedron. The exact
symbolic calculation finds one structural-zero coefficient in the
degree-four Bernstein expansion of $P_A$. For $R_B$, the three
degree-six expansions have respectively $10$, $5$, and $10$ structural
zeros; for $R_C$, they have $5$, $10$, and $10$. These coefficients
vanish identically over $\mathbb Q(b,c,d,e)$. Every remaining coefficient
has a strictly positive exact rational interval enclosure at each stated
level. Table~\ref{tab:n4certificate} reports the smallest positive lower
bounds by region. Factor endpoints lie on a dyadic grid of width
$2^{-120}$, their binomial-CDF signs are checked with integer arithmetic,
and all polynomial, vertex, and Bernstein calculations thereafter use
exact rational arithmetic. The symbolic and numerical layers are
regenerated by \texttt{make n4-structure-check} and
\texttt{make n4-certificate-check}. Hence $P_A,R_B,R_C\ge0$, and
\eqref{eq:n4capgoal} follows in all four regions. Validity of $G_\alpha$
then proves the coverage statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The case \texorpdfstring{$n=5$}{n=5}}

At $n=5$ the normalized knot domain is four-dimensional and all five cap
regions require polynomial certificates. The exact computation is larger,
but the comparison principle is unchanged.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n5}
Let $n=5$ and let $T_1,\ldots,T_5$ be i.i.d.\ with common distribution $F$
on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. If
$\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$, then
\[
\SB\ge G_\alpha(T_1,\ldots,T_5)
\quad\text{for every observed sample},
\qquad
\Pr_F(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the binomial Stringer bound is distribution-free conservative at
$90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ nominal confidence when $n=5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $p_j=p_5(j)$ and put
\[
a=1-p_4,\quad b=p_4-p_3,\quad c=p_3-p_2,\quad
d=p_2-p_1,\quad e=p_1-p_0,\quad f=p_0.
\]
For ascending taints the Stringer bound is
$at_1+bt_2+ct_3+dt_4+et_5+f$. After affine normalization, it is enough to
consider
\[
(0,x,y,z,w,1),\qquad 0\le x\le y\le z\le w\le1,
\qquad s=bx+cy+dz+ew+f.
\]
With $(D_0,\ldots,D_5)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)$, define
\[
V=\Pr_D\{xD_1+yD_2+zD_3+wD_4+D_5>s\}.
\]
Put
\[
\begin{split}
H_1&=\frac{(1-s)^5}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)(1-w)},\\
H_w&=\frac{(w-s)^5}{w(w-x)(w-y)(w-z)(1-w)},\\
H_z&=\frac{(z-s)^5}{z(z-x)(z-y)(w-z)(1-z)},\\
H_y&=\frac{(y-s)^5}{y(y-x)(z-y)(w-y)(1-y)}.
\end{split}
\]
The uniform-simplex cap formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n5caps}
V=\begin{cases}
H_1,&s\ge w,\\
H_1-H_w,&z\le s\le w,\\
H_1-H_w+H_z,&y\le s\le z,\\
H_1-H_w+H_z-H_y,&x\le s\le y,\\
1-s^5/(xyzw),&s\le x,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with boundary values defined by continuity. If
$q_1=e+f$, $q_2=d+e+f$, $q_3=c+d+e+f$, and
$g=b+c+d+e+f$, the factor equations imply
\begin{align}
\alpha&=(1-f)^5,\nonumber\\
\alpha&=(1-q_1)^4(1+4q_1),\nonumber\\
\alpha&=(1-q_2)^3(1+3q_2+6q_2^2),\label{eq:n5identities}\\
\alpha&=(1-q_3)^2(1+2q_3+3q_3^2+4q_3^3),\nonumber\\
1-\alpha&=g^5.\nonumber
\end{align}

Let $K=(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)(1-w)$,
$W=w(w-x)(w-y)(w-z)$, $Z=z(z-x)(z-y)$, and $Y=y(y-x)$.
In the first four regions, multiply $\alpha-V$ by, respectively,
$K,KW,KWZ,$ and $KWZY$, and denote the cleared residuals by
$P_A,P_B,P_C,P_D$. For the final region use
$P_E=s^5-g^5xyzw$. Exact polynomial division after
\eqref{eq:n5identities} gives
\begin{align*}
P_B&=(1-w)R_B,\\
P_C&=(1-w)(1-z)(w-z)R_C,\\
P_D&=(1-w)(1-z)(1-y)(w-z)(w-y)(z-y)R_D.
\end{align*}
The remaining polynomials $P_A,R_B,R_C,R_D,P_E$ have degrees
$5,8,9,8,5$, respectively, and every extracted factor is nonnegative.

For completeness, we describe the exact four-simplex decomposition on
which their Bernstein coefficients are evaluated. Let

intersection of the edge $v_iv_j$ with $s=x_r$. Successive slicing by

\endgroup
The listed simplices meet only on common faces and cover their respective

Map each four-simplex affinely to the standard four-simplex. The respective

\end{proof}

\subsection{The case \texorpdfstring{$n=6$}{n=6}}

The next dimension has six cap regions. A larger exact certificate proves
all three conventional levels.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n6}
Let $n=6$ and let $T_1,\ldots,T_6$ be i.i.d.\ with common distribution $F$
on $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu$. For
$\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$,
\[
\SB\ge G_{\alpha}(T_1,\ldots,T_6)
\quad\text{for every observed sample},
\qquad
\Pr_F(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the binomial Stringer bound is distribution-free conservative at
$90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$ nominal confidence when $n=6$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $p_j=p_6(j)$ and put
\[
\begin{split}
a&=1-p_5,\quad b=p_5-p_4,\quad c=p_4-p_3,\quad
d=p_3-p_2,\\
e&=p_2-p_1,\quad f=p_1-p_0,\quad g=p_0.
\end{split}
\]
If the smallest taint is one, all taints, Stringer, and the Gaffke limit
equal one. Otherwise, subtracting the smallest taint and dividing by its
distance from one gives the ascending knots and Stringer threshold
\[
\begin{gathered}
(t_0,\ldots,t_6)=(0,x,y,z,w,u,1),\qquad
0\le x\le y\le z\le w\le u\le1,\\
s=bx+cy+dz+ew+fu+g.
\end{gathered}
\]
For $(D_0,\ldots,D_6)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)$, let
\[
V=\Pr_D\!\left\{\sum_{i=0}^6t_iD_i>s\right\}.
\]
For distinct knots, the uniform-simplex cap formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n6cap}
V=\sum_{i:t_i>s}\frac{(t_i-s)^6}
 {\prod_{j\ne i}(t_i-t_j)},
\end{equation}
and coincident-knot values follow by continuity. It is enough to prove
$V\le\alpha$ at each listed value of $\alpha$.

For $k=0,\ldots,4$, set
\[
q_0=g,\quad q_1=f+g,\quad q_2=e+f+g,\quad
q_3=d+e+f+g,\quad q_4=c+d+e+f+g.
\]
The Clopper--Pearson equations give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n6identities}
\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{6}{j}q_k^j(1-q_k)^{6-j}
\quad(k=0,\ldots,4),
\qquad
1-\alpha=(b+c+d+e+f+g)^6.
\end{equation}
In the first five regions of \eqref{eq:n6cap}, clear the positive common
denominator in $\alpha-V$ and substitute \eqref{eq:n6identities}; in the
last region use
$s^6-(b+c+d+e+f+g)^6xyzwu$. Exact polynomial division extracts the
following nonnegative boundary factors:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{region}&\text{residual degree}&\text{extracted factors}\\ \hline
s\ge u&6&1\\
w\le s\le u&10&1-u\\
z\le s\le w&12&(1-w)(1-u)(u-w)\\
y\le s\le z&12&(1-z)(1-w)(1-u)(w-z)(u-z)(u-w)\\
x\le s\le y&10&\displaystyle
 \prod_{r\in\{y,z,w,u\}}(1-r)
 \prod_{r<r';\ r,r'\in\{y,z,w,u\}}(r'-r)\\
s\le x&6&1.
\end{array}
\]
The divisions are performed in an exact polynomial ring and reject any
nonzero remainder.

Let $v_i=(0^{,5-i},1^i)$, $i=0,\ldots,5$, be the ordered-knot simplex
vertices. Intersecting its edges with $s=u,w,z,y,x$ and slicing
successively gives fixed triangulations containing respectively
$1,5,10,10,5,1$ five-simplices. Every intersection coordinate is retained
as an exact rational function of $b,\ldots,g$. Exact boundary identities
and directed strict-sign checks place every vertex in its claimed convex
region, and all 210 pairs of the 21 named vertices are certified as
distinct. The oriented-chain check
pairs all 48 internal four-facets with opposite orientations. The 96
unpaired four-facets lie on the six outer ordered-simplex hyperplanes, 16
on each, and the directed determinant sum lies strictly between $1/2$ and
$3/2$. Thus the chain represents an integer multiple of the generator of
$H_5(\Delta,\partial\Delta;\mathbb Z)$, and its determinant sum is that
integer times the normalized volume of $\Delta$, which is one. The integer
is therefore one. Because every simplex has positive orientation and lies
in $\Delta$, the 32 simplices cover the domain without interior
multiplicity. After mapping each five-simplex to the standard
five-simplex, the six residual degrees, simplex counts, and total
structural-zero counts are
\[

\section{Poisson factors at conventional audit confidence levels}
\label{sec:poisson}

For $0\le j\le n$, let $p_n^{\mathrm B}(j)$ denote the binomial factor
used above, and let
\[
p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)=\frac{\lambda_j}{n},
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{j}e^{-\lambda_j}\frac{\lambda_j^i}{i!}=\alpha,
\]
denote its Poisson analogue. The Poisson factors are not truncated at
one; as in audit factor tables, a factor may exceed one.

\begin{proposition}[All-$n$ factor domination]
\label{prop:poissondomination}
If $0<\alpha<e^{-1}$, then
\[
p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)\ge p_n^{\mathrm B}(j),
\qquad j=0,\ldots,n,\quad n\ge1.
\]
Consequently, the Poisson-factor Stringer bound is at least the
binomial-factor Stringer bound for every observed sample.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $B(j;n,p)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\le j\}$ and
$P(j;\lambda)=\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\le j\}$.
Anderson and Samuels \cite[Corollary~2.1 and Section~4.2]{andsam1967}
show that
\[
B(j;n,\lambda/n)<P(j;\lambda)
\quad\text{when }j<\lambda-1\text{ and }\lambda<n.
\]
Because $P(j;\lambda_j)=\alpha<e^{-1}=P(0;1)$, their
Theorem~4.1 gives $\lambda_j>j+1$. If $\lambda_j<n$, the displayed
inequality yields
$B(j;n,p_n^{\mathrm P}(j))<\alpha$; since $B(j;n,p)$ is decreasing in
$p$, its root satisfies
$p_n^{\mathrm B}(j)<p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)$. If $\lambda_j\ge n$, the
conclusion follows instead from
$p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)\ge1\ge p_n^{\mathrm B}(j)$.

For the pointwise comparison, put
$d_j=p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)-p_n^{\mathrm B}(j)\ge0$. If
$t_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge t_{(n)}$, summation by parts gives
\[
\SB_{\mathrm P}-\SB_{\mathrm B}
=d_0(1-t_{(1)})
 +\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_j(t_{(j)}-t_{(j+1)})
 +d_n t_{(n)}\ge0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:poissoncoverage}
The Poisson-factor Stringer bound is distribution-free conservative
at $n=2$ whenever $0<\alpha<e^{-1}$, and at $n=3$, $n=4$, $n=5$, and
$n=6$ for $\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:n2}, \ref{thm:n3}, \ref{thm:n4}, \ref{thm:n5}, and
\ref{thm:n6}.
\end{proof}

The factor comparison is not the only route to a Poisson guarantee. The
following lemma uses a simultaneous survival band with the index required
at the right side of each sample point.

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous-band lower bound]\label{lem:poissonband}
Let $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ be i.i.d.\ with common distribution $F$ on $[0,1]$
and mean $\mu$, and let $t_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge t_{(n)}$ be the observed order
statistics. Let $0\le c_0\le\cdots\le c_n$ with $c_n\ge1$, and form
\[
L_c=c_0+\sum_{j=1}^n(c_j-c_{j-1})t_{(j)}.
\]
Put $a_i=\min\{1,c_{i-1}\}$ and let
\[
Q_n(a_1,\ldots,a_n)
=\Pr\{V_{i:n}\le a_i,\ i=1,\ldots,n\},
\]
where $V_{1:n}\le\cdots\le V_{n:n}$ are uniform order statistics. Then
\[
\Pr_F\{L_c\ge\mu\}\ge Q_n(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
\]
If $\bar c_j=\min\{1,c_j\}$ and $L_{\bar c}$ is formed from those
factorwise-capped coefficients, then the same lower bound holds and
$L_{\bar c}\le\min\{1,L_c\}$ pointwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $G(x)=\Pr_F\{T>x\}$ and
$N_x=\#\{r:T_r>x\}$. Apart from the finitely many sampled values, which
do not affect the integral,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integral-band}
L_c=\int_0^1c_{N_x}\,dx,
\qquad \mu=\int_0^1G(x)\,dx.
\end{equation}
To include atomic $F$, augment the experiment with independent
$R_r\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$ and define
\[
V_r=\Pr_F\{T>T_r\}+R_r\Pr_F\{T=T_r\}.
\]
The randomized probability integral transform makes the $V_r$ i.i.d.
uniform. If $N_x=j<n$, the $j$ observations above $x$ have $V_r\le G(x)$
and the remaining observations have $V_r\ge G(x)$. Hence
$G(x)\le V_{j+1:n}$. On the event in the definition of $Q_n$, this gives
$G(x)\le a_{j+1}\le c_j$. If $N_x=n$, use $G(x)\le1\le c_n$.
Integration in \eqref{eq:integral-band} proves the event inclusion and
hence the result. The coverage event depends only on the original sample,
so its probability in the augmented experiment is its ordinary sampling
probability. The capped factors are nondecreasing, end at one, and induce
the same boundaries $a_i$. The first conclusion therefore applies to them.
Finally, summation by parts gives
\[
L_c=c_0(1-t_{(1)})+
\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_j(t_{(j)}-t_{(j+1)})+c_nt_{(n)}.
\]
All displayed weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Since
$\bar c_j\le c_j$ and $\bar c_n=1$, this representation gives
$L_{\bar c}\le L_c$ and $L_{\bar c}\le1$.
\end{proof}

For nondecreasing boundaries $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, Bolshev's recursion
\cite[pp.~366--367]{shorackwellner1986} evaluates the probability in the
lemma exactly. With $Q_0=1$, it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bolshev-corrected}
Q_m=1-\sum_{i=1}^m\binom mi(1-a_{m-i+1})^iQ_{m-i},
\qquad m=1,\ldots,n.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Direct Poisson guarantees at larger sample sizes]
\label{thm:poissonband}
Let the observations be i.i.d.\ with an arbitrary common distribution $F$
on $[0,1]$. The Poisson-factor Stringer bound is conservative throughout
the following ranges:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{nominal confidence}&\text{every certified sample size}\\ \hline
90\%&1\le n\le8\\
95\%&1\le n\le11\\
99\%&1\le n\le20.
\end{array}
\]
The conclusion also holds if the Poisson factors are capped at one before
forming the bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband} with
$c_j=p_n^{\mathrm P}(j)=\lambda_j/n$; these factors are increasing in $j$.
At the listed levels,
$\alpha<e^{-1}$, so $\lambda_n>n+1$ by the argument in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination}; hence $c_n>1$.

For each required $j$, the certificate encloses $\lambda_j$ between
adjacent dyadic rationals $L_j<U_j$ on the grid $2^{-80}\mathbb Z$.
The endpoint signs
\[
e^{-L_j}\sum_{k=0}^j\frac{L_j^k}{k!}>\alpha,
\qquad
e^{-U_j}\sum_{k=0}^j\frac{U_j^k}{k!}<\alpha
\]
are checked with exact rational bounds for the exponential. Thus
$L_j<\lambda_j<U_j$. Substitution of the smaller boundaries
$a_i^- =\min\{1,L_{i-1}/n\}$ into
\eqref{eq:bolshev-corrected} gives an exact rational lower bound for the
event probability in Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband}. The certificate checks
every positive $n$ in the three stated ranges. At the largest $n$ its
lower bounds are as follows; the displayed decimals summarize exact
rationals.

All arithmetic in this certificate is exact. After power-of-two range
reduction to $[0,1]$, consecutive odd and even partial sums of the
alternating exponential series enclose $e^{-x}$; the code then evaluates
\eqref{eq:bolshev-corrected} with rational arithmetic. At the next sample
size, even the upper endpoint brackets give simultaneous-event probabilities
$0.8903656999$, $0.9482646529$, and $0.9899786984$, respectively, each
below nominal. This limits the sufficient-event proof, not the Stringer
bound: the integral inequality can hold when the pointwise band crosses
$G$.

The same sufficient event gives an all-sample-size calibration while
retaining the familiar Poisson factor curve. For $0<\alpha<1$, define
$\lambda_j$ as above and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappaband}
\begin{split}
\kappa_0&=\max\left\{1,\frac n{\lambda_n}\right\},\\
a_i(\kappa)&=\min\left\{1,\frac{\kappa\lambda_{i-1}}n\right\},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,\\
Q_{n,\alpha}(\kappa)
&=Q_n\bigl(a_1(\kappa),\ldots,a_n(\kappa)\bigr),\\
\kappa_{n,\alpha}
&=\inf\{\kappa\ge\kappa_0:Q_{n,\alpha}(\kappa)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The function $Q_{n,\alpha}$ is continuous and nondecreasing; its superlevel
set in \eqref{eq:kappaband} is therefore closed.

\begin{theorem}[All-sample-size scalar Poisson calibration]
\label{thm:poissoncalibration}
Let the observations be i.i.d.\ with an arbitrary common distribution on
$[0,1]$, and let $0<\alpha<1$. Then
\[
U_{n,\alpha}
=\min\{1,\kappa_{n,\alpha}\SB_{\mathrm P}\}
\]
has coverage at least $1-\alpha$ for every $n\ge1$. The same conclusion
holds with any certified $\kappa\ge\kappa_{n,\alpha}$. It also holds if each
Poisson factor is capped at one before the complete factor-capped Stringer
expression is multiplied by $\kappa$. A pointwise no-larger valid rule uses
the calibrated factors
\[
\widehat c_j=\min\left\{1,\frac{\kappa\lambda_j}{n}\right\}
\]
directly in the Stringer formula; the same curve results if the ordinary
factors are capped before calibration.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying every Poisson factor by $\kappa$ multiplies the complete
Stringer expression by $\kappa$. Thus use Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband} with
$c_j=\kappa\lambda_j/n$. These factors are nondecreasing, and
$c_n\ge1$ for every $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$. The lemma and \eqref{eq:kappaband} give
\[
\pr\{\kappa\SB_{\mathrm P}\ge\mu\}
\ge Q_{n,\alpha}(\kappa).
\]
The set in \eqref{eq:kappaband} is nonempty: because the $\lambda_j$
are increasing, $\kappa=\max\{1,n/\lambda_0\}$ belongs to the permitted
domain, makes every $a_i(\kappa)$ equal to one, and hence makes
$Q_{n,\alpha}(\kappa)=1$. Continuity shows that the
infimum is attained. Use $\kappa=\kappa_{n,\alpha}$ in the preceding
inequality. Finally, capping at one preserves coverage because $\mu\le1$.

For the factor-capped convention, put
$\bar p_j=\min\{1,\lambda_j/n\}$ and use $c_j=\kappa\bar p_j$.
Because $\kappa\ge1$,
\[
\min\{1,c_{i-1}\}
=\min\left\{1,\frac{\kappa\lambda_{i-1}}n\right\}
=a_i(\kappa),
\]
and the definition of $\kappa_0$ gives $c_n\ge1$. Thus the same event and
the same argument apply. Finally, apply the factorwise-capping conclusion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband}. Because $\kappa\ge1$,
$\min\{1,\kappa\bar p_j\}=\widehat c_j$, so both base-factor conventions
give the stated common curve, pointwise no larger than their final-cap
reports.
\end{proof}

The exact joint calibration in \eqref{eq:kappaband} is readily precomputed,
but the next result gives a closed-form upper bound that does not depend on
the sample size.

\begin{corollary}[Sample-size-uniform multiplier]
\label{cor:poissonuniform}
Suppose $0<\alpha\le e^{-1}$ and $\kappa>1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformkappa}
\alpha^{\kappa-1}\le 1-\kappa e^{1-\kappa}.
\end{equation}
Then $\kappa_{n,\alpha}\le\kappa$ for every $n\ge1$. Consequently,
both the final-cap and calibrated-factor-capped full-scale rules formed with
this $\kappa$ have coverage at least $1-\alpha$ at every sample size. In
particular,
\[
\kappa_{n,\alpha}\le2
\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad
\alpha\le1-\frac2e.
\]
Thus the multiplier two is a distribution-free valid choice, uniformly in
$n$, at every nominal confidence level at least
$2/e\approx73.5759\%$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $\lambda_0=-\log\alpha$ and let
$X_a\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a,1)$. Because $\alpha\le e^{-1}$,
$c=\lambda_0-1\ge0$. The gamma-tail monotonicity theorem of
Pinelis \cite{pinelisgamma2021} states that
$\Pr\{X_a-a>c\}$ is increasing in $a$. Hence, for every integer $j\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammashift}
\Pr\{X_{j+1}>j+\lambda_0\}
\ge \Pr\{X_1>\lambda_0\}=\alpha,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \lambda_j\ge j+\lambda_0.
\end{equation}
Here we used the Erlang--Poisson identity
$\Pr\{X_{j+1}>x\}=P(j;x)$ and the definition $P(j;\lambda_j)=\alpha$.
In particular, the terminal condition in Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband}
holds for every $\kappa>1$.

Put $\rho=\kappa e^{1-\kappa}<1$. If
$a_{j+1}(\kappa)=\kappa\lambda_j/n<1$, the
Anderson--Samuels comparison used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\{V_{j+1:n}>a_{j+1}(\kappa)\}
&=B\left(j;n,\frac{\kappa\lambda_j}{n}\right)\\
&<P(j;\kappa\lambda_j)\\
&\le \alpha\,\kappa^j e^{-(\kappa-1)\lambda_j}
 \le \alpha^\kappa\rho^j.
\end{aligned}
\]
The comparison applies because \eqref{eq:gammashift}, $\lambda_0\ge1$,
and $\kappa>1$ imply $j<\kappa\lambda_j-1$. The first weak inequality in
the last two lines uses
$\sum_{r=0}^j\kappa^r\lambda_j^r/r!
 \le\kappa^j\sum_{r=0}^j\lambda_j^r/r!$; the last uses
\eqref{eq:gammashift}. If the boundary is one, the probability on the left
is zero. A union bound therefore yields
\[
1-Q_{n,\alpha}(\kappa)
\le \alpha^\kappa\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\rho^j
\le \frac{\alpha^\kappa}{1-\rho}
\le\alpha
\]
by \eqref{eq:uniformkappa}. Thus $\kappa$ belongs to the defining set in
\eqref{eq:kappaband}. The coverage statements follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:poissoncalibration}. For $\kappa=2$,
\eqref{eq:uniformkappa} reduces to $\alpha\le1-2/e$.
\end{proof}

The full-scale rule raises the no-error factor. An anchored calibration can
leave that factor unchanged. Put
$\bar p_j=\min\{1,\lambda_j/n\}$. If $\bar p_0<1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:etaband}
\begin{split}
\eta_0&=\max\left\{1,
\frac{1-\bar p_0}{\bar p_n-\bar p_0}\right\},\\
d_j(\eta)&=\bar p_0+\eta(\bar p_j-\bar p_0),\\
b_i(\eta)&=\min\{1,d_{i-1}(\eta)\},\\
R_{n,\alpha}(\eta)
&=Q_n\bigl(b_1(\eta),\ldots,b_n(\eta)\bigr),\\
\eta_{n,\alpha}
&=\inf\{\eta\ge\eta_0:R_{n,\alpha}(\eta)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
If $\bar p_0=1$, set $\eta_{n,\alpha}=1$.

\begin{corollary}[Zero-taint-preserving Poisson calibration]
\label{cor:poissonanchor}
Let $\SB_{\bar{\mathrm P}}$ use the factor-capped curve $\bar p_j$, and
let $p_j^{\mathrm P}=\lambda_j/n$ denote the untruncated factors. Both
\[
\begin{split}
U^{\mathrm A}_{n,\alpha}
&=\min\{1,\bar p_0+\eta_{n,\alpha}
(\SB_{\bar{\mathrm P}}-\bar p_0)\},\\
U^{\mathrm A,u}_{n,\alpha}
&=\min\{1,p_0^{\mathrm P}+\eta_{n,\alpha}
(\SB_{\mathrm P}-p_0^{\mathrm P})\}
\end{split}
\]
have coverage at least $1-\alpha$ for every $n\ge1$ and every
$0<\alpha<1$. Moreover,
$\eta_{n,\alpha}\ge\kappa_{n,\alpha}$. On an all-zero sample, each returns
its ordinary zero-taint factor before the final cap. The same coverage holds
after capping every effective anchored factor at one; the capped and
untruncated base curves then give the same factor sequence and a report
pointwise no larger than either displayed rule.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\bar p_0=1$, monotonicity gives $\bar p_j=1$ for every $j$, while
$p_0^{\mathrm P}\ge1$. Moreover, $\kappa_0=1$ and
$Q_{n,\alpha}(1)=1$, so
$\kappa_{n,\alpha}=\eta_{n,\alpha}=1$. Both displayed rules equal one after
the final cap. Hence assume $\bar p_0<1$.
The factors $d_j(\eta)$ are nondecreasing and
$d_n(\eta)\ge1$ on the permitted domain, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband} gives coverage at least
$R_{n,\alpha}(\eta)$. The feasible set in \eqref{eq:etaband} is nonempty.
Indeed, every boundary except the first tends to one as
$\eta\to\infty$, whereas $b_1(\eta)=\bar p_0$. In the nontrivial case,
$\bar p_0=\lambda_0/n<1$ and $\lambda_0=-\log\alpha$, so
\[
\lim_{\eta\to\infty}R_{n,\alpha}(\eta)
=1-(1-\lambda_0/n)^n
>1-e^{-\lambda_0}=1-\alpha.
\]
Continuity shows that the infimum is finite and attained. Linearity of the
Stringer formula gives the first displayed rule.

For the untruncated curve, use
$d_j^{u}=p_0^{\mathrm P}+\eta(p_j^{\mathrm P}-p_0^{\mathrm P})$.
If $p_j^{\mathrm P}<1$, it has the same capped boundary as $d_j$; if
$p_j^{\mathrm P}\ge1$, both boundaries equal one because $\eta\ge1$.
The terminal condition also holds. Thus the same event proves the second
rule. Capping either effective factor curve at one preserves the event and
gives identical factors, so the final assertion follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:poissonband}. If $\bar p_n<1$, then
\[
\eta_0=\frac{1-\bar p_0}{\bar p_n-\bar p_0}
\ge\frac1{\bar p_n}=\kappa_0;
\]
if $\bar p_n=1$, both domain lower bounds equal one. For every
$x\ge\eta_0$,
\[
b_i(x)\le \min\{1,x\bar p_{i-1}\}=a_i(x),
\]
so $R_{n,\alpha}(x)\le Q_{n,\alpha}(x)$. Minimality therefore gives
$\eta_{n,\alpha}\ge\kappa_{n,\alpha}$. If every taint is zero, all
factor-increment terms vanish.
\end{proof}

The anchored multiplier is never smaller, but it applies only to the error
increments. The calibration path must be
pre-specified; taking the smaller of the two bounds after observing the
sample is not justified by these results. At $95\%$ confidence, simple
six-decimal valid anchored multipliers at $n=25,50,100,$ and $200$ are
$1.511563$, $1.947538$, $2.367560$, and $2.778290$, respectively; exact

For the full-scale path, an explicit analytic upper bound is available at

\]
and define
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm B}=\max\left\{\kappa_0,
\max_{0\le j<n}\frac{\lambda_j(\theta)}{\lambda_j}\right\}.
\]
For $b_i=\min\{1,\lambda_{i-1}(\theta)/n\}$, the
Anderson--Samuels comparison used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} gives
\[
\pr(V_{i:n}>b_i)
=\pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,b_i)\le i-1\}\le\theta.
\]
If $\lambda_{i-1}(\theta)\ge n$, the probability is zero; otherwise the
comparison applies directly.
The union bound gives
$\pr(V_{i:n}\le b_i\ \forall i)\ge1-n\theta=1-\alpha$.
Since $a_i(\kappa_{\mathrm B})\ge b_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappabound}
\kappa_{n,\alpha}\le\kappa_{\mathrm B}<\infty.
\end{equation}

probability. In an implementation, the underlying Poisson factors must also
be exact or conservatively rounded; upward rounding of the scalar alone does
not control factor-table rounding.

Thus the factor comparison previously checked numerically at $90\%$ and
$95\%$ is analytic and holds at every sample size. In particular,
Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} implies finite-sample conservatism of the
Poisson-factor Stringer bound at $n=2$ throughout this confidence range.
Theorems~\ref{thm:n3}, \ref{thm:n4}, \ref{thm:n5}, and \ref{thm:n6} give
the same conclusion from $n=3$ through $n=6$ for the three certified
conventional levels. Theorem~\ref{thm:poissonband} extends the proved
Poisson ranges to $n=8$, $n=11$, and $n=20$ at $90\%$, $95\%$, and
$99\%$, respectively.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poissoncalibration} supplies a conservative
scalar-calibrated rule beyond those ranges without claiming coverage for the
ordinary Poisson bound.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poissonuniform} supplies closed-form full-scale
multipliers that are valid simultaneously for every sample size.
More generally, any distributional class for which the binomial-factor
bound is proved conservative inherits the same conclusion for the
Poisson-factor bound. Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} does not,
by itself, settle coverage in the remaining cases, because the
binomial-factor conjecture remains unresolved there.

\section{An all-sample-size reporting safeguard}\label{sec:safeguard}

The valid comparison bound also gives a directly computable fallback
outside the sample sizes for which ordinary Stringer has been proved.
For $M\in\{\mathrm B,\mathrm P\}$, define
\[
\SB^{\mathrm{safe}}_{M,\alpha}(t_1,\ldots,t_n)
=\max\{\SB_{M,\alpha}(t_1,\ldots,t_n),
        G_\alpha(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Stringer--Gaffke safeguard]\label{prop:safeguard}
Let $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ be independent $[0,1]$-valued observations with a
common mean $\mu$. For every $n\ge1$, every $\alpha\in(0,1)$, and either
factor convention $M$,
\[
\Pr\{\SB^{\mathrm{safe}}_{M,\alpha}\ge\mu\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For $n\in\{3,4,5,6\}$ and $\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.01\}$, the
safeguarded value equals the ordinary Stringer value pointwise for both
factor conventions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite-sample validity of the Gaffke limit gives
$\Pr\{G_\alpha(T_1,\ldots,T_n)\ge\mu\}\ge1-\alpha$. Since the maximum is
pointwise at least $G_\alpha$, the first conclusion follows. At the listed
sample sizes and levels, Theorems~\ref{thm:n3}, \ref{thm:n4},
\ref{thm:n5}, and \ref{thm:n6} give
$\SB_{\mathrm B,\alpha}\ge G_\alpha$ on every sample.
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination} gives
$\SB_{\mathrm P,\alpha}\ge\SB_{\mathrm B,\alpha}$, proving the second
conclusion.
\end{proof}

The proposition validates the pre-specified maximum, not ordinary Stringer
in cases where Stringer is smaller. The repository implementation evaluates
the Dirichlet-average tail at rational points by exact confluent divided
differences and returns an exact-sign dyadic upper bracket for $G_\alpha$.
Thus the operational floor does not depend on trusting a floating-point
B-spline calculation.

\subsection{An all-sample-size one-cap region}\label{sec:onecap}

The safeguard is known to be identical to Stringer on every sample at
$n=3$, $n=4$, $n=5$, and $n=6$ at the three certified levels. The following
result identifies a directly checkable identity region without a sample-size
cutoff.

\begin{proposition}[One-cap comparison]\label{prop:onecap}
Let $n\ge1$ and $0<\alpha\le 1/4$. Arrange the observed taints as
$x_0\le\cdots\le x_{n-1}$, and let $s_{\mathrm B}$ be their
binomial-factor Stringer value. If
$s_{\mathrm B}\ge x_{n-1}$, then
\[
s_{\mathrm B}\ge G_\alpha(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}).
\]
Consequently, on that sample the pre-specified safeguard equals ordinary
Stringer for both the binomial and Poisson factor conventions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first give an analytic cap bound with no restriction on $n$. Let
$x_n=1$, let $c_i>0$ with $\sum_{i=0}^n c_i=1$, and put
\[
s=\sum_{i=0}^n c_ix_i,\qquad
C_r=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}c_i\quad(1\le r\le n).
\]
For $D\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)$, if
$s\ge x_{n-1}$, the one-upper-knot cap formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onecapformula}
V:=\Pr_D\!\left\{\sum_{i=0}^nx_iD_i>s\right\}
=\frac{(1-s)^n}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-x_i)}.
\end{equation}
Degenerate boundary cases follow directly or by continuity. Otherwise set
$S=1-s$, $y_i=1-x_i$, and $u_i=y_i/S-1$. Then
\[
u_0\ge\cdots\ge u_{n-1}\ge0,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_iu_i=c_n,\qquad
V=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1+u_i)^{-1}.
\]
Writing $u_n=0$ and letting $v_r$ denote the vector with its first $r$
entries equal to one and the rest zero, define
\[
\lambda_r=\frac{C_r(u_{r-1}-u_r)}{c_n}.
\]
The $\lambda_r$ are nonnegative, sum to one, and
\[
u=\sum_{r=1}^n\lambda_r\frac{c_n}{C_r}v_r.
\]
Since $\sum_i\log(1+u_i)$ is concave, its value on this convex hull is at
least its smallest vertex value. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onecapbound}
V\le\max_{1\le r\le n}
\left(\frac{C_r}{C_r+c_n}\right)^r.
\end{equation}

For the ascending-knot binomial Stringer representation,
\[
c_0=1-p_n(n-1),\quad
c_j=p_n(n-j)-p_n(n-j-1)\ (1\le j<n),\quad
c_n=p_n(0),
\]
so $C_r=1-p_n(n-r)$. The $r=n$ term in
\eqref{eq:onecapbound} equals
$(1-p_n(0))^n=\alpha$. For every $r<n$, every
$n$, and $0<\alpha\le 1/4$, we now prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onecapfactor}
\left(
\frac{1-p_n(n-r)}{1-p_n(n-r)+p_n(0)}
\right)^{r}\le\alpha.
\end{equation}

Write $\alpha=e^{-x}$, $a=e^{-x/n}$, and $b=e^{-x/r}$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qbar}
q_{n,r}=\frac{b(1-a)}{1-b}
=\frac{1-e^{-x/n}}{e^{x/r}-1}.
\end{equation}
By binomial symmetry, $q=1-p_n(n-r)$ is the unique solution of
$\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\ge r\}=\alpha$. Inequality
\eqref{eq:onecapfactor} is equivalent to $q\le q_{n,r}$. It therefore
suffices to show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qbartail}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,q_{n,r})\ge r\}\ge\alpha.
\end{equation}

First suppose $r\ge2$, and put $\mu_n=nq_{n,r}$. For fixed $r$,
$\mu_n$ increases with $n$, because
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\{z(1-e^{-x/z})\}
=1-(1+x/z)e^{-x/z}>0.
\]
Also $q_{n,r}<r/n$, because the numerator in \eqref{eq:qbar} is less
than $x/n$ and the denominator is greater than $x/r$. If
$\mu_n\ge r-1$, then a binomial variable $X$ with this mean satisfies
$\{X\ge r\}=\{X>\E X\}$. Since
$nq_{n,r}\ge1>\log(4/3)$, the sharp binomial-mean inequality of Pinelis
\cite{pinelis2021} gives
\[
\Pr(X\ge r)\ge\frac14\ge\alpha.
\]
If instead $\mu_n<r-1$, let $\mu_0=\mu_{r+1}$. Monotonicity in the
success probability, followed by the fixed-mean binomial monotonicity of
Anderson and Samuels \cite[Theorem~2.1]{andsam1967}, gives
\begin{align}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,q_{n,r})\ge r\}
&\ge\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,\mu_0/n)\ge r\}\notag\\
&\ge\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(r+1,q_{r+1,r})\ge r\},
\label{eq:fixedmeanreduce}
\end{align}
because $r-1>\mu_0$.

It remains to bound the last probability. Set
\[
u=\frac{x}{r+1},\qquad v=\frac{x}{r},\qquad
A=\frac{1-e^{-u}}{1-e^{-v}},
\]
so $q_{r+1,r}=bA$ and $\alpha=b^r$. The two-term boundary tail is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundarytail}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(r+1,bA)\ge r\}
=\alpha A^r(r+1-rbA).
\end{equation}
Write
\[
M_r(x)=A^r(r+1-rbA),
\]
the multiplier of $\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:boundarytail}. This multiplier is
strictly increasing in $x$. Indeed, with
$\psi(z)=z/(e^z-1)$,
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\log A=\frac{\psi(u)-\psi(v)}x>0.
\]
Moreover, $\psi$ is decreasing and
\[
\psi'(z)+1
=\frac{e^z(e^z-1-z)}{(e^z-1)^2}>0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\log(bA)
=-\frac1r+\frac{\psi(u)-\psi(v)}x
<-\frac1r+\frac{v-u}{x}=-\frac1{r+1}<0.
\]
Thus both factors defining $M_r(x)$ increase with $x$.

It remains to prove $M_r(1)>1$. Put $t=1/r$ and
$d=t^2/(1+t)$. At $x=1$,
\[
1-A=\frac{e^d-1}{e^t-1}=: \delta.
\]
For $0<z<2$, the elementary bounds
\[
z+\frac{z^2}{2}<e^z-1<\frac{z}{1-z/2},
\qquad
1-z<e^{-z}<1-z+\frac{z^2}{2}
\]
follow from the exponential series and
$2\operatorname{arctanh}(z/2)>z$. They give
\[
\delta<\frac{d/(1-d/2)}{t+t^2/2},
\qquad
\delta>\frac{d(1-t/2)}t.
\]
Since $(e^z-1)/z$ is increasing, we also have
$\delta<d/t=1/(r+1)$ and hence $A>r/(r+1)$. The second bound, together
with $b=e^{-t}>1-t$ and $1-b>t-t^2/2$, gives
\begin{align*}
r+1-rbA
&=1+r(1-b)+rb\delta\\
&>1+\left(1-\frac t2\right)
+\frac{(1-t)(1-t/2)}{1+t}
=\frac3{1+t}=\frac{3r}{r+1}.
\end{align*}
Therefore, for $r\ge5$,
\[
M_r(1)>3\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^{r+1}
\ge3\left(\frac56\right)^6>1,
\]
because the sequence $(r/(r+1))^{r+1}$ is increasing. For the remaining
cases, the first bound on $\delta$ gives the following exact rational lower
margins:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&A\text{ is greater than}&
\dfrac{3r}{r+1}A^r-1\text{ is greater than}\\ \hline
2&39/55&17/3025\\
3&125/161&885001/16693124\\
4&287/351&1841131309/25297477335.
\end{array}
\]
Thus $M_r(1)>1$ for every $r\ge2$. Because
$x\ge\log4>1$ throughout the stated range, the boundary probability in
\eqref{eq:fixedmeanreduce} is at least $\alpha$.

For $r=1$, direct calculation completes the argument. In this case
\[
q_{n,1}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}(1-\alpha^{1/n}),
\qquad
nq_{n,1}\ge\frac{2\alpha}{1+\sqrt\alpha}
\ge\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\ge-\log(1-\alpha),
\]
where the middle inequality holds for $\alpha\le1/4$, as it does throughout
the stated range. Therefore
\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,q_{n,1})\ge1\}
=1-(1-q_{n,1})^n\ge1-e^{-nq_{n,1}}\ge\alpha.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:qbartail}, hence \eqref{eq:onecapfactor}, for all
$n$ in the stated confidence range.

Thus \eqref{eq:onecapbound} is at most $\alpha$, which gives
$G_\alpha\le s_{\mathrm B}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination}
gives $s_{\mathrm P}\ge s_{\mathrm B}$ throughout this range, proving the
claim for both safeguarded conventions.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a pointwise identity statement for the pre-specified
safeguard, not a conditional-coverage theorem for ordinary Stringer. The
analytic proof has no finite sample-size cutoff. The repository retains the
earlier $59{,}700$ exact dyadic comparisons through $n=200$ at $90\%$,
$95\%$, and $99\%$ as an independent regression, not as the logical basis
of the result. Controlling the remaining cap regions is a separate open
problem.

\section{Exact counterexamples at low confidence}\label{sec:counter}

Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} covers every confidence level, so the asymptotic
anti-conservatism at $\alpha>1/2$ \cite{papvz1996} must manifest itself
only at larger $n$. Before giving explicit finite-sample counterexamples, we
exclude the smallest possible nontrivial support.

\begin{proposition}[One nonzero taint value]\label{prop:twopoint}
If $F$ is supported on $\{0,v\}$ with $v\in(0,1]$, then, for every
$n$ and $\alpha$, $\pr(\SB\ge\mu)\ge1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $K$ denote the number of nonzero taints. Then
$K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)$,
\[
\SB=(1-v)p_n(0)+v\,p_n(K)\ge v\,p_n(K),
\qquad \mu=qv.
\]
The Clopper--Pearson coverage event
$\{p_n(K)\ge q\}$ has probability at least $1-\alpha$; on that
event, $\SB\ge vq=\mu$.
\end{proof}

Each counterexample below is certified: its distributional parameters are
exact dyadic rationals, its coverage is computed as an exact rational by
full multinomial enumeration, and every comparison $\SB\ge\mu$ is made
with rational factor intervals. Their endpoints lie on a dyadic grid with
denominator $2^{80}$, and the sign of the binomial CDF at each endpoint is
evaluated exactly with integer arithmetic.
The table rows are regenerated from the version-2 certificate files by
\path{summarize_certificates.py}.

\section{Reassessment of a previous finite-sample certification}\label{sec:bimpeh}

Let
\[
C_n(F)=\pr_F\bigl(\SB\ge\mu(F)\bigr)
\]
denote the coverage probability. In this section,
$t_{1:n}\le\cdots\le t_{n:n}$ are the sample taints arranged in
nondecreasing order, with $t_{0:n}=0$ and $t_{n+1:n}=1$. Let $q_n(i)$
be the lower one-sided Clopper--Pearson limit for $i$ successes, with
$q_n(0)=0$. The lower and upper limits used here satisfy
$q_n(i)=1-p_n(n-i)$.

Bimpeh \cite[pp.~76--79]{bimpeh2008} writes the Stringer bound as
$\SB=\int_0^1[1-\widehat F_{n,L}(t)]\,dt$, where the stepwise band is
defined by
\[
\widehat F_{n,L}(t)=q_n(i-1)
\quad\text{for }t_{i-1:n}\le t<t_{i:n},\qquad 1\le i\le n+1.
\]
Let $V_{1:n}\le\cdots\le V_{n:n}$ denote the order statistics of $n$
independent $\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$ random variables. Using the
probability integral transform and the reversal symmetry of uniform
order statistics, Bimpeh proposes the lower bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bimpeh}
C_n(F)\;\ge\;\bar P_n
\;:=\;\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\le p_n(i),\ 1\le i\le n\bigr).
\end{equation}
Bimpeh evaluates $\bar P_n$ using Bolshev's recursion for uniform
order-statistic boundary-crossing probabilities
\cite[pp.~366--367]{shorackwellner1986}. Because the computed values
satisfy $\bar P_n\ge1-\alpha$ for $n\le11$ when $\alpha=0.05$, the
thesis concludes that \eqref{eq:conj} holds in that range. It also notes
that
\[
\bar P_2=2\sqrt{1-\alpha}-(1-\alpha)\ge1-\alpha
\]
for every $\alpha$ and claims a proof for $n\le2$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bimpehwrong}
Inequality \eqref{eq:bimpeh} fails for both continuous and atomic
distributions $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha=0.05$ and let $F$ be the mixture assigning probability
$0.99$ to a uniform distribution on $(0.99,1)$ and probability $0.01$
to a uniform distribution on $(0,0.01)$, so $\mu=0.9851$. For $n=1$,
$\SB=0.95+0.05\,T_1$ and $\bar P_1=1$. Moreover,
$\SB<\mu$ if and only if $T_1<0.702$, an event of probability $0.01$.
Thus $C_1(F)=0.99<1=\bar P_1$. For $n=2$,
$\bar P_2=2\sqrt{0.95}-0.95\approx0.99936$. A sample containing one
taint from each mixture component fails, as does a sample containing
two taints from the lower component. Therefore
$C_2(F)=1-2(0.99)(0.01)-0.01^2=0.9801<\bar P_2$.

For an atomic example at $n=5$, let
$\pr(T=1)=\tfrac12$ and
$\pr(T=\tfrac1{10})=\pr(T=0)=\tfrac14$. Its exact coverage is
$C_5(F)=\tfrac{31}{32}=0.96875<\bar P_5\approx0.98746$. The failure
event is precisely the event of drawing no taint-$1$ unit, which has
probability $2^{-5}$.
\end{proof}

The issue is an index shift. Pointwise domination
$F\ge\widehat F_{n,L}$ on $[0,1]$ requires
$F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)$ for all $i$, because $q_n(i)$ is the band's
value at the jump point $t_{i:n}$. The cited argument instead uses the
left limit $q_n(i-1)$. In particular, it omits the constraint
$F(t_{n:n})\ge q_n(n)=\alpha^{1/n}$. Correcting the index does not
recover the claimed coverage guarantee.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cannot}
For every continuous $F$ and every $n$, the corrected containment
probability satisfies
$\pr\bigl(F(t_{i:n})\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)
=\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)\le1-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For continuous $F$, the probability integral transform gives
$F(t_{i:n})=V_{i:n}$ in distribution. The corrected event includes its
$i=n$ constraint, and $q_n(n)=\alpha^{1/n}$. Hence
\[
\pr\bigl(V_{i:n}\ge q_n(i)\ \forall i\bigr)
\le \pr\bigl(V_{n:n}\ge\alpha^{1/n}\bigr)=1-\alpha,
\]
because $\pr(V_{n:n}<x)=x^n$.
\end{proof}

Thus the corrected pointwise-containment event has probability no
greater than nominal coverage. The integral inequality
$\int\widehat F_{n,L}\le\int F$ may hold on a larger event than
pointwise domination, but the cited containment argument does not
establish that fact. Our implementation reproduces the values of
$\bar P_n$ and Bimpeh's Table~5.1 exactly; those values are
boundary-crossing probabilities, not established coverage bounds.
Consequently, the cited argument does not establish finite-sample
conservatism at $n=2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:n2} supplies that result directly.

\section{Discussion}

Three observations suggest directions for future work. First, the reformulation behind
\eqref{eq:red} generalizes: with $u_{(1)}\le\cdots\le u_{(n)}$ the
increasing order statistics of $U_i=1-T_i$,
\[
\SB\;=\;1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_j\,u_{(j)},\qquad
e_j=p_n(j)-p_n(j-1),
\]
so \eqref{eq:conj} is a family of weighted order-statistic exceedance
inequalities, with the audit-specific confidence-factor structure
encoded in the positive increments $e_j$. Second, the $n=2$ proof
combines rectangle lower bounds on the covering event, a
survival-integral budget, and a concave potential with equal endpoint
values. The $n=3$ through $n=6$ proofs use a different route: they compare
Stringer with a valid Dirichlet-average bound and certify the resulting
simplex-cap inequalities. At $n=6$ the exact computation certifies all three
conventional levels. In higher
dimensions, the analogous pointwise comparison is a concrete geometric
problem for caps of a uniform simplex.
Proposition~\ref{prop:onecap} solves the complete one-upper-knot region at
every sample size whenever nominal confidence is at least
$75\%$. For the
binomial factors at $90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$, $n=7$ is the first sample
size with unresolved cap regions. A
dimension-free route recorded in the
repository writes the Poisson
bound as an ordered interpolation of gamma quantiles and reduces its
pointwise Gaffke comparison to two high-quantile inequalities for weighted
exponentials and uniform Dirichlet averages. Those inequalities remain
conjectural. Third, the near-equality
observed at every searched $n$, together with the certified failures at
several nominal confidence levels below $50\%$, suggests that
\eqref{eq:conj} may hold whenever nominal confidence is at least $50\%$,
equivalently whenever $\alpha\le1/2$, with coverage infimum $1-\alpha$
at every $n$. This is a concrete conjecture for future work.

From an auditing perspective, the binomial Stringer bound has no uniform
safety margin above nominal coverage: at $n=2$, its coverage infimum is
$1-\alpha$. Theorems~\ref{thm:n3}, \ref{thm:n4}, and \ref{thm:n5}
supply exact guarantees at the three conventional levels for $n=3$,
$n=4$, and $n=5$; Theorem~\ref{thm:n6} adds all three guarantees at
$n=6$. At conventional confidence levels, the Poisson version
pointwise dominates the binomial version by
Proposition~\ref{prop:poissondomination}. The corrected simultaneous-band
argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:poissonband} extends the rigorous Poisson
guarantees to every $n\le8$ at $90\%$, every $n\le11$ at $95\%$, and every
$n\le20$ at $99\%$ confidence; the reported Poisson searches at larger
sizes retain a positive but decreasing numerical cushion.
For those larger sizes, Theorem~\ref{thm:poissoncalibration} supplies a
second distribution-free option that stays within the Poisson Stringer
factor family: multiply the complete result by the precomputed
$\kappa_{n,\alpha}$ and cap at one, or use the pointwise no-larger
calibrated-factor-capped curve. This is a guarantee for the calibrated rule,
not evidence about coverage of the ordinary rule.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poissonuniform} gives a closed-form alternative at
conventional confidence levels: $1.76$, $1.66$, and $1.52$ are valid at
every sample size for $90\%$, $95\%$, and $99\%$, respectively. More
generally, the multiplier two works whenever nominal confidence is at least
$2/e$. The exact joint calibration is appreciably smaller in the reported
examples.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poissonanchor} gives a second calibration path that
preserves the ordinary all-zero-sample result and scales only the error
increments. The path must be chosen before the sample is observed. The
certified examples show that
anti-conservatism can occur at several nominal confidence levels below
$50\%$, at sample sizes $n=50,100,200,$ and $400$. Coverage for ordinary
audit sample sizes at conventional confidence remains an open problem, so
the search evidence alone does not justify a general validity claim for
ordinary Stringer. Proposition~\ref{prop:safeguard}, however, supplies an
all-sample-size distribution-free reporting option under the model of this
paper: pre-specify the maximum of the familiar Stringer calculation and the
validated Gaffke floor. This fallback requires professional-standards and
workflow evaluation before practice adoption, but it does not require the
unresolved Stringer conjecture. Proposition~\ref{prop:onecap} additionally
proves that the fallback has zero uplift at every sample size and nominal
confidence level of at least $75\%$ whenever binomial
Stringer is at least the largest observed taint.
