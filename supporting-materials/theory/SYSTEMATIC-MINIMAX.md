# Exact finite-frame minimax inversion for systematic PPS

## Mathematical status

**Status.** The design-coverage and dominance theorems below are proved by
ordinary mathematics. The solver implements their finite rational LP
representation exactly. Its committed certificate is regenerated by
`make systematic-minimax-check`.

**One-sentence thesis.** On a fixed known systematic-PPS frame, inversion of
the exact lower-tail randomization rank of the grid mean gives an
identity-aware upper bound that is design-valid and never exceeds either the
sharp taint-only Markov bound or the deterministic completion bound; when the
phase atoms partition the frame, the nuisance optimization has exact
closed-form and subset-sum specializations.

The term *minimax* here has a precise and limited meaning: for the fixed
grid-mean test ordering, the bound maximizes the target over every compatible
least-favourable completion of the unknown taints. It does **not** claim
optimality among all possible confidence procedures or all possible test
orderings.

## 1. Fixed-frame randomization distribution

Fix the ordered book weights and the intended sample size. Exact phase
enumeration reduces the uniform random start to finitely many distinct phase
atoms $a=1,\ldots,K$, with probabilities $p_a>0$ satisfying
$\sum_a p_a=1$. Let $m_{ai}$ be the number of times item $i$ is hit in
atom $a$. For a complete taint vector $u\in[0,1]^N$, write

$$
Y_a(u)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^N m_{ai}u_i,
\qquad
\theta(u)=\frac{\sum_iw_iu_i}{W}.
$$

Repeated hits are retained in $m_{ai}$. Atoms with identical incidence
vectors may be combined by adding their probabilities.

Suppose the observed phase audits the distinct item set $S$, returns
taints $x=(x_i)_{i\in S}$, and has grid mean $y$. For any complete
population $u$, define its exact lower-tail randomization rank at that
threshold by

$$
F_u(y)=\sum_{a=1}^Kp_a\mathbf 1\{Y_a(u)\le y\}.
$$

The exact finite-frame bound is

$$
B^{\rm ff}_\alpha(S,x)
=\max\left(
\left\{\theta(u):
 0\le u_i\le1,
 u_i=x_i\ (i\in S),
 F_u(y)>\alpha
\right\}\cup\{0\}
\right).
\tag{FF}
$$

Thus a candidate completion remains in the inverted confidence set exactly
when the observed mean is not in a lower tail of probability at most
$\alpha$ under that candidate's own randomization distribution. The added
zero specifies the output when the inverted set is empty.

## 2. Coverage and pointwise dominance

### Theorem 1 (exact finite-frame validity)

For every fixed ordered frame and every taint vector $t\in[0,1]^N$,

$$
\Pr_R\{B^{\rm ff}_\alpha(S_R,t_{S_R})<\theta(t)\}\le\alpha.
$$

**Proof.** Let $Y_R(t)$ be the observed grid mean and let
$F_t(z)=\Pr_R\{Y_R(t)\le z\}$. If the bound is below $\theta(t)$, the
true completion $u=t$ cannot be feasible in (FF), so
$F_t(Y_R(t))\le\alpha$. For any discrete random variable $Y$,

$$
\Pr\{F_Y(Y)\le\alpha\}\le\alpha:
$$

if the event is nonempty, its probability is the CDF at the largest support
point whose CDF is at most $\alpha$. Applying this elementary rank lemma to
$Y_R(t)$ proves the claim. $\square$

Let

$$
M_\alpha=1-\alpha+\alpha y,
\qquad
C(S,x)=\frac{\sum_{i\in S}w_ix_i+\sum_{i\notin S}w_i}{W}.
$$

### Theorem 2 (dominance of both closed-form fallbacks)

For every observation,

$$
B^{\rm ff}_\alpha(S,x)\le
\min\{M_\alpha,C(S,x)\}.
$$

**Proof.** Every compatible completion has target at most $C(S,x)$, so
the completion inequality is immediate. For the Markov inequality, take a
feasible completion $u$ and let $Y$ have the phase distribution
$Y_a(u)$. Exact design unbiasedness gives $\mathbb E Y=\theta(u)$. If
$y<1$, then

$$
F_u(y)
=\Pr\{1-Y\ge1-y\}
\le\frac{1-\theta(u)}{1-y}.
$$

Because feasibility requires $F_u(y)>\alpha$, this gives
$\theta(u)<1-\alpha(1-y)=M_\alpha$. The phase set is finite, so the feasible
set in (FF) is a finite union of compact polytopes and its maximum is
attained. Hence $B^{\rm ff}_\alpha\le M_\alpha$. For $y=1$, both sides
are at most one. $\square$

Consequently

$$
\max\{\min(1,\operatorname{SB}_\alpha),B^{\rm ff}_\alpha\}
$$

is a design-valid Stringer overlay, never below capped ordinary Stringer and
never above the earlier overlay based on
$\min\{M_\alpha,C(S,x)\}$. This is a strict improvement on some frames.

## 3. Exact LP representation

For a phase coalition $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}$ with
$\sum_{a\in A}p_a>\alpha$, consider the rational linear program

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize} & W^{-1}\sum_iw_iu_i,\\
\text{subject to} & 0\le u_i\le1,\\
&u_i=x_i\quad(i\in S),\\
&n^{-1}\sum_i m_{ai}u_i\le y\quad(a\in A).
\end{array}
\tag{LP(A)}
$$

The condition $F_u(y)>\alpha$ holds if and only if the low-mean atoms
contain such a coalition. Therefore

$$
B^{\rm ff}_\alpha
=\max_{A:\,p(A)>\alpha}\operatorname{value}\{\operatorname{LP}(A)\}.
$$

Only inclusion-minimal coalitions need be retained: every larger coalition
contains a minimal one, and deleting constraints cannot lower an LP maximum.
This proves the solver architecture. An equivalent formulation uses binary
coalition variables in a MILP; the implementation instead enumerates the
minimal coalitions and solves the resulting LPs by exact rational simplex.
It thereby avoids treating a floating-point MILP optimum or gap as a proof.
For each nontrivial LP, it checks primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and
exact equality of the two rational objectives.

All inputs consumed by the exact implementation are rational. Phase
probabilities come from rational item weights, and rational observed taints
give rational LP data and rational optimal vertices. Unknown equal-weight
items with identical phase-incidence columns are aggregated into a bounded
sum variable; the solver expands the exact group sums back to a complete
taint witness before accepting the result. It then checks the observation,
coalition inequalities, target, exact lower-tail mass, and both dominance
inequalities. Coalition enumeration is exponential in the worst case, so
explicit coalition and search-node limits raise an exception before any
partial result can be reported as certified.

The generic command-line interface accepts exact integers, decimal strings,
or rational strings. Raw phase indices are zero-based in the order returned
by exact phase enumeration. For example:

```json
{
  "weights": ["1", "1", "1", "1"],
  "sample_size": 1,
  "observed_phase_index": 0,
  "observed_taints": {"0": "1/2"},
  "alpha": "1/4"
}
```

```sh
uv run --frozen python \
  supporting-materials/computations/python/systematic_minimax.py \
  --input frame.json --out result.json
```

The result records the exact bound, both dominance comparators, coalition
counts, maximizing coalition, lower-tail mass, and a complete rational taint
witness. `--omit-witness` suppresses only the potentially long witness list;
the solver still constructs and checks it internally.

## 4. Phase-partition formulas and exact complexity

When the atom supports partition the items, the target decomposes as

$$
\theta(u)=\sum_a p_aY_a(u).
$$

If phase $j$ is observed with mean $y$, put

$$
\rho_j(\alpha)=\min\{p(A):j\in A,\ p(A)>\alpha\}.
$$

The exact bound then simplifies to

$$
B^{\rm ff}_\alpha(j,y)=1-(1-y)\rho_j(\alpha).
$$

For $m$ equal phases this is

$$
B^{\rm ff}_\alpha(j,y)
=1-\frac{\lfloor m\alpha\rfloor+1}{m}(1-y).
$$

For rational unequal masses, denominator clearing reduces $\rho_j$ to a
minimum-excess subset sum. An exact $O(KD)$-time, $O(D)$-memory dynamic
program computes it, where $D$ is the scaled total mass. A reduction from
SUBSET SUM proves weak NP-hardness under binary encoding even for a one-draw
all-zero observation. The same DP handles unequal-phase all-zero observations
from independent starts by testing the product-tail threshold exactly.

For $r$ independent starts on $m$ equal disjoint phases, if $d$ distinct
phases are observed and every observed taint is zero, the exact product-design
bound is

$$
1-\frac1m\max\left\{d,\lfloor m\alpha^{1/r}\rfloor+1\right\}.
$$

Complete proofs, the DP recurrence, the hardness reduction, and the exact
implementation boundary are in
[`SYSTEMATIC-DISJOINT-PHASES.md`](SYSTEMATIC-DISJOINT-PHASES.md).

## 5. A 2,000-item, 100-draw certificate

Take 2,000 unit-book-value items, $n=100$, and systematic interval 20.
The 20 integer random-start phases have probability $1/20$ each and select
disjoint sets of 100 items. Suppose one phase is observed and all 100 taints
are zero at 95% confidence.

The earlier two components agree:

$$
M_{0.05}=0.95,
\qquad
C(S,0)=\frac{1900}{2000}=0.95.
$$

For a compatible completion to have $F_u(0)>0.05$, at least two phase
means must be zero. Nonnegative taints then force all 200 items in those two
phases to be zero. Choosing the observed phase and one additional phase, and
setting every other taint to one, attains target

$$
B^{\rm ff}_{0.05}=\frac{1800}{2000}=0.90.
$$

The equal-phase formula gives the value immediately. The general solver
independently enumerates all $\binom{20}{2}=190$ minimal coalitions and
returns exactly this witness, while the specialized dynamic program returns
the same minimum mass. The witness lower-tail mass is $2/20=0.10$. Thus
the exact finite-frame inversion lowers the valid fallback by five percentage
points. On the all-zero sample, both ordinary 95% Stringer outputs are below
0.03, so the design-valid binomial and Poisson overlays both report exactly
0.90 rather than 0.95. These are exact rational comparisons; decimals are
display only.

## 6. Mathematical yield and verification boundary

The obstacle is not bias but identification. A one-start grid has a correct
mean over random starts, yet the observed phase alone leaves many complete
taint populations possible. The universal Markov rule protects against all
such populations without using the frame, while deterministic completion
protects against all unobserved taints without using how other starts would
sample them. Each discards a different part of the available design
information.

The new step intersects those information sources through randomization
inversion. A candidate population must both match the audited item identities
and make the observed grid mean nonexceptional under every possible start.
The finite phase distribution turns that requirement into a union of
polytopes. Exact coalition enumeration and rational LPs therefore solve the
least-favourable nuisance problem without weakening the coverage statement or
replacing it by simulation.

What transfers is the architecture: enumerate the exact design support,
choose and justify a pre-specified test ordering, invert its exact rank, and
maximize the estimand over compatible completions. Different orderings,
strata, overlapping-phase multiple-start observations, or successive-PPS
designs require new validity and optimization arguments. The proved
multiple-start specialization covers phase-partitioned frames only at an
all-zero observation. The present theorem uses only the grid-mean ordering
and does not establish global optimality among all confidence rules.

The proof boundary is explicit:

* ordinary mathematics proves the rank inversion, design coverage,
  dominance, and the coalition-LP representation;
* Python enumerates exact random-start atoms and minimal coalitions;
* SymPy's rational simplex returns exact primal and dual LP vertices; the
  script checks both feasible sets and equality of their objectives, then
  expands and independently checks a complete least-favourable taint witness;
  and
* `systematic-minimax-certificate.json` records the 2,000-item benchmark and
  a nonbinary four-item rational fixture; and
* `systematic_disjoint_dp.py` performs the denominator-cleared exact
  subset-sum recurrence, while `systematic-disjoint-dp-certificate.json`
  records equal, unequal, hardness-reduction, and independent-start finite
  fixtures.

No statement here covers unknown or erroneous book weights, negative taints,
nonrandom starts, a data-dependent choice of test ordering, nonzero
multiple-start observations, or frames whose LP coalition enumeration and
specialized scaled-mass DP both exceed their explicit exact-solver limits.
